<h2 id="national">National Important Days in October 2026 (India)</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Oct 2, Gandhi Jayanti:</strong> marks the birth anniversary of Mahatma Gandhi (1869) and is also the birth anniversary of former PM Lal Bahadur Shastri.</li>
<li><strong>Oct 5, World Teachers&rsquo; Day:</strong> UNESCO's global counterpart to India's own September 5 Teachers&rsquo; Day, recognising the role of educators worldwide.</li>
<li><strong>Oct 8, Indian Air Force Day:</strong> marks the founding of the Indian Air Force in 1932; air shows and flypasts are typically held at IAF stations.</li>
<li><strong>Oct 15, World Students&rsquo; Day:</strong> observed on the birth anniversary of Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam, India's former President and the "Missile Man of India."</li>
<li><strong>Oct 21, Police Commemoration Day:</strong> honours police personnel who lost their lives in the line of duty, tracing back to the 1959 Hot Springs clash in Ladakh.</li>
<li><strong>Oct 31, National Unity Day:</strong> celebrates the birth anniversary of Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (1875), architect of India's political integration, and is marked with the Run for Unity across the country.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="deep-dive">Key Days of October 2026, Explained</h2>

<h3>Gandhi Jayanti: October 2</h3>
<p>India observes the birth anniversary of Mahatma Gandhi as a national holiday, with prayer meetings, tributes at Raj Ghat, and cleanliness drives under the Swachh Bharat mission. The United Nations separately marks the same date as the International Day of Non-Violence, adopted by the General Assembly in 2007 in recognition of Gandhi's principle of Ahimsa.</p>

<h3>World Teachers&rsquo; Day: October 5</h3>
<p>Established by UNESCO in 1994, this day commemorates the adoption of the 1966 ILO/UNESCO Recommendation on the Status of Teachers. It highlights the working conditions and professional standing of teachers as a distinct observance from India's own Teachers&rsquo; Day on September 5.</p>

<h3>World Habitat Day: October 5</h3>
<p>Held on the first Monday of October, this UN-Habitat day reflects on the state of towns and cities and the basic right to adequate shelter. <strong>The 2026 theme is &ldquo;Adequate Housing for All,&rdquo;</strong> spotlighting the global housing crisis that leaves an estimated 2.8 billion people facing some form of housing inadequacy.</p>

<h3>World Mental Health Day: October 10</h3>
<p>Founded by the World Federation for Mental Health in 1992, this day raises awareness of mental health issues and mobilises support for prevention and treatment. Schools, workplaces and health bodies typically run screening camps and awareness drives around this date.</p>

<h3>Navratri and Dussehra: October 12 to 20</h3>
<p>Shardiya Navratri begins with Ghatasthapana on Monday, October 12, and spans nine nights of worship dedicated to the Navadurga, the nine forms of Goddess Durga. The festival closes with Vijayadashami, popularly known as Dussehra, on Tuesday, October 20, commemorating Lord Rama's victory over Ravana and Goddess Durga's victory over the demon Mahishasura.</p>

<h3>World Food Day: October 16</h3>
<p>Marking the 1945 founding of the UN Food and Agriculture Organization, World Food Day draws global attention to hunger, malnutrition and sustainable agriculture, with events coordinated in over 150 countries.</p>

<h3>United Nations Day: October 24</h3>
<p>This day marks the anniversary of the UN Charter's entry into force in 1945, following ratification by a majority of its founding members. It is observed alongside World Polio Day, which raises funds and awareness for global polio eradication efforts led by Rotary International and the WHO.</p>

<h3>National Unity Day: October 31</h3>
<p>India marks the birth anniversary of Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, the first Deputy Prime Minister and Home Minister, credited with the political integration of over 560 princely states into the Indian Union after independence. The Statue of Unity in Gujarat, the world's tallest statue, is dedicated to him.</p>

<div class="correction" id="corrections">
<h2 style="border:none;margin-top:0;">Date Corrections: What Other &ldquo;Important Days&rdquo; Lists Get Wrong for 2026</h2>
<p>Several October observances follow a rule (&ldquo;first Monday,&rdquo; &ldquo;second Saturday&rdquo;) or a lunar-calendar date rather than a fixed one, so the exact day changes every year. Checked against the real October 2026 calendar and Hindu Panchang:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Diwali is not in October 2026 at all.</strong> Because of the Adhik Maas (extra lunar month) running from May 17 to June 15, 2026, the entire post-monsoon festival calendar has shifted about three weeks later than usual. Diwali 2026 falls on <strong>Sunday, November 8</strong>, with Dhanteras around November 5 to 6. Any October 2026 list that includes Diwali has not accounted for this leap month.</li>
<li><strong>World Habitat Day</strong> is the first Monday of October. In 2026 that is <strong>October 5</strong>, not October 6, which a number of recycled calendar pages state (October 6, 2026 is a Tuesday).</li>
<li><strong>World Migratory Bird Day</strong>'s October leg falls on the second Saturday of the month, correctly <strong>October 10, 2026</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>World Statistics Day</strong> is fixed at October 20, but it is only an official UN observance once every five years (2010, 2015, 2020, 2025, then 2026). 2026 is not one of those years, even though some sites list it as an annual event.</li>
</ul>
</div>
