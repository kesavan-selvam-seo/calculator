How age is calculated

The calculator compares your date of birth with the selected target date using calendar years, months and days. If the target day is earlier than the birth day, the calculation borrows days from the previous calendar month. If the resulting month difference is negative, it borrows one year.

This is more accurate than simply subtracting the birth year from the target year because exact age depends on whether the birthday has already occurred.

Age calculator for exams, jobs and admissions

Many applications do not ask for your age “today”. They specify a cutoff date such as 1 January, 1 July or the last date of application. To check eligibility, enter the date of birth from your official record and use the exact cutoff date stated in the notification.

Important: This calculator gives a calendar-based age result. Minimum/maximum age limits, relaxations, inclusive dates and February 29 rules can be defined differently by an authority. Always use the official notification for the final eligibility decision.

Age in years, months, days, weeks and total days

The main result shows completed calendar years, months and remaining days. The additional breakdown shows total elapsed days, completed weeks and completed calendar months. These figures answer slightly different questions, so they should not be mixed.

  • Years, months and days: best for age statements and cutoff-date checks.
  • Total days: the number of calendar days elapsed between the two dates.
  • Total weeks: completed seven-day periods based on total days.
  • Completed months: completed calendar months in the exact age result.

Which generation am I? Gen Z, Millennial, Gen X and more

Your birth year is often used to place you in a broad generational cohort. These labels are useful for general discussion, but they are not official age categories and the cutoff years can vary between researchers and publishers.

Silent Generation1928–1945

A commonly used research range for people born before the post-war baby boom.

Baby Boomers1946–1964

The U.S. Census-linked baby-boom years are widely treated as a distinct cohort.

Generation X1965–1980

A commonly used range between the Baby Boomers and Millennials.

Millennials1981–1996

Pew Research Center uses 1981 through 1996 for the Millennial generation.

Generation Z1997–2012*

This calculator starts Gen Z at 1997. The 2012 endpoint is a practical convention; exact endpoints vary.

Generation Alpha2013 onward*

Used here as an informal label for the cohort after Gen Z; naming and boundaries are still less standardized.

Generation note: Pew Research Center defines Millennials as 1981–1996 and starts the next cohort in 1997, while also noting that generational boundaries are not an exact science. CalcuPortal uses the ranges above only as a simple reference label, not as a scientific or legal classification. Read Pew’s generation methodology.

Leap years and February 29 birthdays

The calculator uses actual calendar month lengths, so leap years are included in the date calculation. People born on February 29 should be especially careful when using the result for legal, exam or institutional eligibility because rules for the effective birthday in a non-leap year can vary.

For general date arithmetic, the calculator follows the calendar dates you enter. For an official age rule, follow the definition used by the relevant authority.

Common age-calculation mistakes

  • Subtracting only the years: 2026 − 2000 is not always age 26; the 2026 birthday may not have happened yet.
  • Using today instead of the cutoff date: applications often specify a separate “age as of” date.
  • Ignoring month lengths: months have 28, 29, 30 or 31 days, so manual borrowing can cause errors.
  • Treating an online result as an official ruling: eligibility may include special age-relaxation or date-counting rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate my exact age from date of birth?

Enter your DOB and the date on which you want to know your age. The calculator returns completed years, completed months and remaining days.

Can I calculate my age on a future date?

Yes. Choose any target date after your date of birth to see how old you will be on that date.

Can I use this for exam or job age eligibility?

You can use it to calculate your calendar age on the stated cutoff date. Always check the official notification for age limits, relaxations and special rules.

Does the calculator handle leap years?

Yes. It uses real calendar month lengths. February 29 birthdays may have special official treatment in non-leap years, so verify the applicable rule when eligibility matters.

What is the difference between age and total days lived?

Age in years, months and days is a calendar breakdown. Total days is the elapsed number of calendar days between the two dates.

Which generation am I based on my birth year?

The calculator shows a common generation label using your birth year. The ranges are approximate, and different sources may use slightly different cutoff years.

Is my date of birth stored?

The age calculation on this page runs in your browser. The entered dates do not need to be sent to a server to produce the result.
