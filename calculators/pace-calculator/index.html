What is a running pace calculator?

A running pace calculator finds the time you spend covering one kilometre or one mile. It can also calculate how long it should take to cover a planned distance at a constant pace, or how far you could travel in a given time. These calculations are useful for reviewing a workout, planning race targets and checking training splits.

CalcuPortal's pace tool supports both kilometres and miles. It displays average pace in min/km and min/mile, converts that pace to speed in km/h and mph, and generates an even-split table and projected times at popular race distances.

Running pace, time and distance formulas

Find pace

Pace = Time ÷ Distance

Example: 30 min ÷ 5 km = 6:00 min/km.

Find finish time

Time = Pace × Distance

Example: 5 min/km × 10 km = 50 min.

Find distance

Distance = Time ÷ Pace

Example: 30 min ÷ 5 min/km = 6 km.

Use consistent units when applying the formulas: a pace in minutes per mile must be multiplied by a distance in miles, not kilometres. The calculator can convert between the two using the exact international-mile definition of 1 mile = 1.609344 km.

Example: 5K running pace for 25 minutes

Suppose you complete a five-kilometre run in 25 minutes. Divide the total 1,500 seconds by five kilometres to get 300 seconds per kilometre. This equals a pace of 5:00 min/km. Multiply by 1.609344 to convert to approximately 483 seconds per mile, or 8:03 min/mile.

Your average speed is 12 km/h, or approximately 7.46 mph. At a perfectly constant pace, you would reach each one-kilometre split every five minutes. Real runs can be faster or slower across different sections.

What are running splits?

A running split is the time recorded over part of a race or workout — such as each kilometre or mile. For even splits, the time for each equal-distance segment is identical. A negative split generally means the later part of a run is faster than the earlier part, while a positive split means the later part is slower.

This calculator produces an even-pace projection based on a single average pace. It shows the cumulative time after each split and a final partial split for non-whole distances, such as 21.0975 km. It does not model hills, water stops, a progressively faster finish or fatigue.

Pace vs speed: minutes per kilometre and km/h

Running pace

Time required for one kilometre or mile, usually written as min/km or min/mile. A smaller value indicates faster running.

Running speed

Distance covered per hour, shown in km/h or mph. A larger value indicates faster movement.

Track pace

For a standard 400-metre distance, multiply seconds per kilometre by 0.4 to estimate a 400 m split at the same average speed.

To convert minutes per kilometre into kilometres per hour, first express pace as seconds per kilometre, then use speed (km/h) = 3,600 ÷ seconds per km. At 5:00 per kilometre, speed is 3,600 ÷ 300 = 12 km/h.

What is a good running pace for a beginner?

There is no universal running pace that is right for everyone. A person's comfortable pace varies with experience, age, training, terrain, weather and individual ability. For someone new to running, a sustainable conversational effort may be more useful than trying to match another person's number.

Use a calculator to understand your existing average pace and plan realistic splits. An even mathematical pace does not establish a safe or achievable goal for a particular runner. Avoid treating estimated finish times as guarantees.

Common running pace calculation mistakes

  • Mixing kilometres and miles: 5:00 per kilometre is not 5:00 per mile; convert using 1.609344 km per mile.
  • Using decimal minutes as seconds: 5.5 minutes is 5 minutes 30 seconds, not 5:50.
  • Confusing pace and speed: lower min/km is faster, while higher km/h is faster.
  • Rounding seconds without carrying: an answer near 4:59.8 rounds to 5:00, not 4:60.
  • Ignoring stoppage time: a moving pace and total elapsed-time pace can differ if you take breaks.
  • Treating split projections as predictions: even-pace tables assume constant speed throughout the event.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate running pace?

Divide elapsed time by distance in matching units. Running 5 km in 25 minutes gives a pace of 5:00 per km.

How do I convert min/km to min/mile?

Multiply pace in seconds per kilometre by 1.609344 to obtain seconds per mile. For example, 5:00 per km is approximately 8:03 per mile.

How do I find finish time from pace?

Multiply seconds per kilometre by kilometres, or seconds per mile by miles. At 6:00 per km, a 10 km run takes 60 minutes at a constant pace.

How far can I run in 30 minutes at 5:00/km?

Thirty minutes divided by five minutes per kilometre equals six kilometres, assuming a consistent average pace.

How long are a half marathon and a marathon?

A half marathon is 21.0975 km and a marathon is 42.195 km. The converter uses those distances in its presets.

Are race finish-time estimates guaranteed?

No. They show what the time would be at the same average pace across another distance; they do not account for fatigue, course difficulty or race conditions.

Is a faster pace a smaller or larger number?

In minutes per kilometre or mile, a smaller number means a faster pace. In kilometres or miles per hour, a larger number means a faster speed.
