What is revenue growth rate?

Revenue growth rate is the percentage increase or decrease in revenue from one period to another. Businesses use it to evaluate sales performance, demand, pricing and expansion. Revenue growth differs from profit growth: a business can sell more while earning less profit if costs rise faster than revenue.

Compare like-for-like reporting periods. For example, compare January 2026 with January 2025 for YoY growth or September 2026 with August 2026 for MoM growth. Make sure the figures are in the same currency, both net or both gross, and use the same rules for refunds, returns and tax.

Revenue growth rate formula

Revenue growth rate (%) = ((Current revenue − Previous revenue) ÷ Previous revenue) × 100

Suppose last year your business generated ₹10,00,000 in revenue and this year it generated ₹12,00,000. The difference is ₹2,00,000. Divide ₹2,00,000 by ₹10,00,000 and multiply by 100 to get 20% YoY revenue growth.

((₹12,00,000 − ₹10,00,000) ÷ ₹10,00,000) × 100 = +20%

What does negative revenue growth mean?

If revenue drops from ₹12,00,000 to ₹9,00,000, the change is −₹3,00,000 and the growth rate is −25%. A negative growth percentage simply indicates that revenue has decreased relative to the selected baseline. It does not directly tell you whether the company is profitable.

Year-over-year (YoY), month-over-month (MoM) and quarter-over-quarter (QoQ)

The percentage change formula is the same across all three comparisons; the difference is which time periods you enter. For YoY, use the same calendar month, quarter or year in successive years. For MoM, use consecutive months; for QoQ, use consecutive quarters. Seasonality can distort MoM and QoQ comparisons, so read the result alongside a longer-term view.

How to calculate revenue CAGR (annualized growth)

The compound annual growth rate (CAGR) represents the constant yearly growth rate that would connect a beginning revenue amount to an ending revenue amount. This is useful when comparing revenue over several years, but it smooths out actual year-to-year volatility and is not the real growth experienced in each individual year.

Revenue CAGR (%) = ((Ending revenue ÷ Beginning revenue)1 ÷ Years − 1) × 100

Example: revenue rises from ₹10 lakh to ₹14.4 lakh over two years. The cumulative increase is 44%, but the annualized CAGR is 20%, because ₹10 lakh × 1.2 × 1.2 = ₹14.4 lakh. For example, moving from 2024 to 2026 represents two elapsed years, not three.

If the starting revenue is zero, CAGR is undefined. The number of years must be positive. The calculator also handles valid revenue declines, including an ending value of zero, which represents −100% CAGR when beginning revenue is positive.

Revenue growth target and future revenue projection

The Revenue target mode applies a fixed growth rate repeatedly. You enter starting revenue, a percentage growth assumption per period, and how many periods to project. The result uses compound growth, rather than simply multiplying a single-period increase by the number of periods.

Projected revenue = Starting revenue × (1 + Growth rate ÷ 100)Number of periods

For instance, starting at ₹1,00,000 and assuming 20% growth for each of three consecutive periods gives ₹1,72,800. This is a mathematical scenario, not a guaranteed forecast. A 20% monthly assumption and a 20% yearly assumption are very different; the time unit and percentage must match.

How to interpret the result accurately

A company with ₹1 crore in revenue last year and ₹1.15 crore this year has 15% growth. If another company increases revenue from ₹10 crore to ₹10.5 crore, it has 5% growth but an absolute increase of ₹50 lakh. Both the percentage and the absolute change matter when evaluating business impact.

A higher revenue growth rate does not automatically mean better profit margin, cash flow or customer retention. Use this figure alongside operating expenses, profit, customers, average revenue per user and other relevant business KPIs. What if previous revenue is zero?

When a new business generates revenue for the first time, the earlier figure might be ₹0. The absolute increase is still measurable (for example, ₹0 to ₹50,000 is +₹50,000), but the growth percentage is mathematically undefined because division by zero is not valid. Avoid presenting an infinite percentage or a made-up 100% growth figure.

Limitations for financial reporting

The calculator does not ingest invoices, verify sales data, adjust for inflation or exchange rates, or distinguish recurring from one-time revenue. It accepts non-negative revenue inputs. If refunds and credits create negative net revenue, consult your organization's financial reporting policy rather than interpreting a conventional growth percentage from a negative baseline.

Frequently asked questions

What is the revenue growth rate formula?

Revenue growth rate = ((current period revenue − previous period revenue) ÷ previous period revenue) × 100, provided previous revenue is greater than zero.

How do I calculate year-over-year revenue growth?

Compare revenue for equivalent periods one year apart, such as full-year 2026 versus full-year 2025. Subtract the earlier revenue from the later revenue, divide by the earlier revenue and multiply by 100.

How do I calculate revenue CAGR?

For a positive initial revenue, CAGR = ((ending revenue ÷ beginning revenue)^(1 ÷ number of years) − 1) × 100. It measures a smoothed annualized growth rate.

What happens if previous revenue is zero?

A percentage growth rate is undefined with zero previous revenue because the denominator would be zero. You can still calculate the absolute revenue increase.

Can revenue growth be negative?

Yes. A revenue drop produces a negative growth percentage. For example, going from ₹1,00,000 to ₹80,000 is a 20% revenue decline.

Does the revenue projection guarantee future results?

No. Compounded projections are mathematical scenarios that assume the same rate each period. Actual revenue depends on business activity, seasonality and other factors.
