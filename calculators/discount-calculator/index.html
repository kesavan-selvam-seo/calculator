What is a discount calculator?

A discount calculator finds how much a price falls during a sale, the amount you save and the final amount payable. It is useful for online shopping, retail sale events, coupon promotions, wholesale price reductions and everyday percentage-off offers. Enter a product's original price and discount rate to see the sale price instantly. You can also compare multi-step discounts and account for a tax rate when appropriate.

Unlike a general percentage calculator, this tool focuses on prices: it shows savings separately from optional tax, combines discounts in the order they apply and supports reverse calculations.

Discount calculator formula

Discount amount = Original price × (Discount % ÷ 100)
Sale price = Original price − Discount amount
Final price including tax = Sale price × (1 + Tax % ÷ 100)

For a single discount, take the percentage of the original price and subtract that amount. If an item costs ₹1,000 with 20% off, you save ₹200 and pay ₹800 before any applicable tax or additional charges.

How to calculate 25%, 40% or 50% off

To find the amount payable, multiply the original price by the remaining percentage. For 25% off, pay 75% of the price; for 40% off, pay 60%; for 50% off, pay half. The example table below uses a ₹1,000 original price without tax.

How to calculate two successive discounts

During a sale, stores often advertise an additional percentage off after the initial discount. Two discounts are applied one after another, not added. A 20% discount followed by another 10% discount reduces a ₹1,000 item to ₹800 and then ₹720. Your combined saving is ₹280, which equals 28% off rather than 30%.

Price after two discounts = Original price × (1 − D1 ÷ 100) × (1 − D2 ÷ 100)

When a flat-value coupon is also available, this calculator deducts the coupon after the two percentage discounts. For example, ₹1,000 with 20% off, then 10% off, then a ₹50 coupon costs ₹670 before tax. Add an illustrative 18% tax on ₹670 to reach ₹790.60. The coupon and tax sequence depends on the actual store and applicable tax rules.

Why 30% + 10% is not 40%

For ₹1,000, the first 30% saves ₹300, leaving ₹700. A further 10% saves ₹70, leaving ₹630. The effective reduction is therefore ₹370, or 37%, not 40%. Compare the final cost rather than adding percentages shown in promotional banners.

How to find discount percentage from two prices

Sometimes you know the original MRP and the advertised sale price, but not the discount percentage. Select Find % off and enter both prices. The calculator shows the markdown percentage and your saving.

Discount % = ((Original price − Sale price) ÷ Original price) × 100

Example: an item originally cost ₹2,000 and is now offered for ₹1,500. The price difference is ₹500, so the discount is (500 ÷ 2,000) × 100 = 25%. The original price must be greater than zero; a higher sale price is a markup, not a discount.

How to find the original price before a discount

Use Find original price when the final sale price and discount percentage are known. The calculator estimates the earlier listed price by reversing the reduction. For example, if ₹800 is the price after 20% off, the original price was ₹800 ÷ 0.80 = ₹1,000.

Original price = Discounted price ÷ (1 − Discount % ÷ 100)

A 100% discount cannot be reversed to a unique original price because many original prices would produce zero. Tax-inclusive final payments should not be entered as the sale price in this reverse mode unless tax is removed first.

Discount, flat coupon, quantity and sales tax

The default calculation assumes a percentage discount is applied to the listed price, then a second percentage discount and optional flat coupon, if selected. Optional tax is estimated on the reduced price. Each per-item stage is rounded to two decimal places before multiplication by the quantity. This mirrors a common checkout approach but may not match every retailer's rounding policy.

For Indian shoppers: MRPs are generally displayed as tax-inclusive prices. Do not add GST again to a tax-inclusive MRP merely because you know a GST rate. If an offer or invoice requires a separate tax calculation, apply the rules relevant to that transaction. The Sales Tax Calculator can help you examine tax-added and tax-inclusive prices separately.

Frequently asked questions about discounts

How do I calculate 20% off a price?

Multiply the original price by 0.20 for the saving and subtract it. A ₹1,000 item at 20% off costs ₹800 before tax, saving ₹200.

Is 20% off plus an extra 10% the same as 30% off?

No. The second 10% applies to the reduced price. The combined discount is 28%, so a ₹1,000 item costs ₹720 before any coupon or tax.

What does 50% off mean?

It means you pay half of the original price. For example, ₹2,400 at 50% off becomes ₹1,200 before any additional tax or charges.

How do I calculate a discount percentage from the MRP and sale price?

Subtract the sale price from the original MRP, divide the difference by the original MRP, then multiply by 100.

How do I reverse a discount to find the original price?

Divide the discounted price by 1 minus the discount rate as a decimal. For example, ₹800 after a 20% discount came from ₹1,000.

Is tax added before or after the discount?

This tool estimates optional tax after the discounts and coupon. Actual tax calculation depends on local laws, whether the quoted price includes tax and the retailer's policies. In particular, do not add GST again to a tax-inclusive MRP.

Can I use this calculator for any currency?

The arithmetic works for any currency. The selector formats results as INR, USD, GBP, EUR or AED without converting exchange rates. Enter all amounts in the same chosen currency.

Why might my store receipt differ slightly?

Retailers may round at a different stage, limit coupons, exclude certain products, calculate taxes differently or have tax-inclusive shelf prices. This calculator rounds each per-item stage to two decimal places and then multiplies by quantity.
