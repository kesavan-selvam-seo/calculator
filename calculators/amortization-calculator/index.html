What is a loan amortization calculator?

A loan amortization calculator shows how a borrowed principal is paid down through periodic installments. For a typical fixed-rate loan, the EMI stays approximately the same each month, but the proportions paid toward interest and principal change over time. Early payments often contain more interest because the outstanding balance is larger. As the balance falls, the interest charged each month decreases and more of the payment goes toward principal.

This calculator produces a complete repayment schedule, an estimate of total interest, and the amount you would repay overall. You can also add an extra payment toward principal each month to estimate interest saved and how much earlier your loan could be closed.

Loan amortization formula and EMI calculation

For a standard fixed-rate reducing-balance loan with monthly payments, calculate the EMI from principal, the monthly rate and the total number of months:

EMI = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n)
P = loan principal · r = annual interest rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100 · n = total monthly installments

For example, a ₹20,00,000 loan at 8.5% annual interest over 15 years has 180 scheduled monthly installments. Each month the lender calculates interest on the outstanding principal at the monthly rate, and the remainder of the payment pays down principal. The last payment can be adjusted slightly to clear rounding differences.

How to calculate amortization at 0% interest

When the annual interest rate is zero, interest is always zero and the EMI becomes Principal ÷ Number of months. For example, ₹1,20,000 over 12 months with 0% interest is ₹10,000 per month. This calculator supports 0% interest instead of producing a divide-by-zero error.

How a monthly amortization schedule works

In each month, the opening loan balance is multiplied by the monthly interest rate to estimate the interest charge. The scheduled payment first pays that interest; the remaining portion reduces the principal. The closing balance becomes the next month's opening balance. You can see all of these components in the month-wise schedule.

Monthly interest = Opening balance × Monthly interest rate
Principal repaid = Total monthly payment − Monthly interest
Closing balance = Opening balance − Principal repaid

The year-wise view groups successive repayment months into 12-month blocks, making it easier to review long home loans. The monthly view displays each individual EMI, while the CSV download saves the complete month-wise schedule, including any extra principal payments.

How extra EMI payments can reduce loan interest

If your loan permits additional principal payments without a penalty, paying more than the scheduled EMI generally reduces the outstanding balance faster. The next month's interest is then calculated on a smaller amount. With the Extra payment per month field, you can compare the original schedule with a shortened payoff schedule and see estimated interest savings.

For example, if you have an existing ₹20 lakh loan and can add ₹5,000 each month, try the extra-payment example and compare the interest saved and repayment months. This model assumes the lender keeps the regular EMI unchanged and reduces the tenure instead of recalculating a lower EMI. Actual loan terms, prepayment restrictions and interest changes can affect the result.

Amortization schedule vs EMI calculator

An EMI calculator typically focuses on the periodic installment and total borrowing cost. An amortization calculator goes further by giving a payment-by-payment schedule showing the outstanding balance after each installment. Both tools use the same core fixed-rate loan mathematics, but the schedule is especially useful when planning prepayments, tracking outstanding principal or estimating the payoff month.

Understanding total interest, payment amounts and loan closure

Loan principal is the amount initially borrowed. Total interest is the sum of monthly interest charges on the reducing balance, excluding fees and taxes. Total repayment is the sum of principal and interest payments. The regular EMI does not include the optional extra monthly principal payment, so your actual monthly outflow can be higher when extra payments are enabled.

For a fixed rate, a longer tenure often lowers the monthly installment but raises total interest paid. A shorter tenure typically increases the EMI while reducing total interest. Compare multiple scenarios to choose a payment amount that fits your budget rather than assuming the lowest EMI is necessarily the cheapest loan.

Important limitations

The schedule is an estimate based on monthly interest calculated from a nominal annual percentage rate divided by 12. Actual lenders may calculate interest daily, change the rate on floating-rate loans, charge fees, include insurance, apply different rounding conventions or limit early repayments. A display currency selection changes formatting only; it does not perform a foreign-exchange conversion. For regulated financial decisions, use your lender's official sanction letter and repayment statement.

Frequently asked questions

What is an amortization schedule?

An amortization schedule lists each loan payment and shows how much goes to interest and principal, along with the remaining loan balance.

How is the monthly EMI calculated?

For a fixed-rate amortizing loan, EMI = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), where P is loan principal, r is monthly interest rate and n is number of monthly payments. At 0% interest, EMI is principal divided by n.

Does paying extra every month reduce loan interest?

Usually yes for a reducing-balance loan. Extra monthly payments applied directly to the principal reduce the outstanding balance and can shorten the loan term and total interest.

Can I calculate an amortization schedule at 0% interest?

Yes. When the annual rate is 0%, each payment goes toward principal, with the final payment adjusted for rounding.

Does this calculator include fees, taxes or floating interest rates?

No. This calculator assumes a fixed nominal annual interest rate, monthly compounding, end-of-month payments, no fees and extra payments that directly reduce principal. Actual lender schedules can differ.

How many months can I calculate?

The calculator supports loan terms from 1 to 480 months and displays a full monthly schedule or a grouped year-by-year summary.
