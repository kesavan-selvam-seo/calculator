How is JEE Main rank estimated?

NTA calculates percentile scores based on the relative performance of candidates within each examination shift. The overall NTA percentile is then used in preparing the merit list. A simple estimate of the number of candidates ahead of a percentile can be calculated using the candidate pool.

Indicative formula
Estimated AIR ≈ ((100 − Overall NTA Percentile) ÷ 100) × Number of unique candidates appeared + 1

This formula is a simplified estimation method. The official AIR can differ because of score precision, tied scores, tie-breaking rules, the final number of unique candidates and NTA's result processing.

Why JEE Main marks do not give one fixed rank

JEE Main Paper 1 is conducted in multiple shifts. NTA converts raw marks into shift-wise NTA percentile scores to account for differences in candidate performance and paper difficulty. As a result, one raw score may correspond to different percentiles in different shifts.

For a more meaningful estimate, use an expected overall percentile from a reliable mock-test analysis or your NTA scorecard. If you only know your raw marks, first use the JEE Main Marks Calculator to calculate your estimated raw score, then refer to shift-specific historical analysis to form a cautious percentile estimate.

What affects your final JEE Main rank?

  • Overall NTA percentile: The percentile is the key input used for the broad AIR estimate.
  • Number of unique candidates: The candidate pool changes the approximate number of candidates ranked ahead.
  • Both sessions: If you appear in both sessions, NTA considers the better total NTA score for the overall merit list.
  • Tie-breaking rules: NTA applies subject-score and other tie-breaking criteria when candidates have the same total NTA score.
  • Final result processing: The official rank is declared by NTA after the examination sessions and result compilation.

Frequently asked questions

How can I predict my JEE Main 2027 rank?

Enter your expected overall NTA percentile and the estimated number of unique candidates who appeared. The tool calculates an indicative AIR using a simplified percentile-to-rank formula.

Can I predict JEE Main rank using marks?

Not accurately with one universal marks-to-rank formula. Raw marks are converted to shift-wise NTA percentile scores, so shift difficulty and candidate performance matter. Use an expected overall percentile for this predictor.

What is the difference between percentile and percentage?

Percentage represents marks obtained out of total marks. Percentile represents the percentage of candidates in the same shift who scored equal to or below a candidate. NTA percentile is not the percentage of marks.

Is the rank shown by this tool official?

No. It is an indicative estimate only. NTA publishes the official Common Rank List and category rank after merging the session NTA scores and applying its tie-breaking rules.

Why does the predictor use 2026 candidate numbers?

The final number of unique candidates who will appear in JEE Main 2027 is not known yet. The 2026 official appeared-candidate count is provided as a reference and can be edited.

Does this tool predict category rank?

No. It estimates overall AIR only. Category rank requires category-specific candidate data and the official NTA result.

Which percentile should I enter if I appeared in both sessions?

For a post-result estimate, use the best overall total NTA percentile considered for merit preparation. For a mock-test estimate, use the expected overall percentile from a comparable test analysis, not the average of subject percentiles.
