JEE Main Inorganic Chemistry Exceptions: Quick Practice

Inorganic chemistry has several important exceptions to general trends. Examples include beryllium having a higher first ionization enthalpy than boron, chlorine having a more negative first electron gain enthalpy than fluorine, and chromium and copper having unusual ground-state configurations. Questions that test the reason behind a trend are more useful than memorising isolated statements.

How to Revise Inorganic Chemistry Exceptions for JEE Main

1. Revise general trends before the exceptions

Start with expected periodic trends in atomic radius, ionization enthalpy and electron gain enthalpy. Then ask which electronic arrangement creates the anomaly. The most useful memory cue is not just “Be exceeds B” but “the electron removed from B comes from a higher-energy 2p orbital.”

2. Use electronic configurations as your explanation

For chromium and copper, write both the expected simple filling pattern and the observed ground-state configuration. For colour and magnetism, check whether d orbitals are empty, full or partially occupied. Do not assume that every d-block ion is coloured or paramagnetic.

3. Connect bonding exceptions to structure

Revisit incomplete-octet species such as BF₃, odd-electron species such as NO, and the molecular orbital explanation for O₂. Bond angles also reflect lone-pair repulsion; for example, the H–O–H angle in water is smaller than the H–N–H angle in ammonia.

4. Compare complexes, not just ligand names

Coordination compounds can have different geometry and magnetic behaviour even when the central metal oxidation state is the same. Compare [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ with [NiCl₄]²⁻ and trace the role of ligand strength rather than memorising the final shapes alone.

5. Practise, review and retry

Start with ten random questions. Read the explanation after each answer, and note why your rejected options fail. Then practise a single weak topic and retry missed questions. The goal is to explain the exception in one sentence, not simply remember the answer letter.
