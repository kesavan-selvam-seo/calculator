How to Calculate BMI: Formula, Chart and Examples

BMI, or Body Mass Index, is a simple number calculated using your height and weight. It is commonly used to understand whether a person falls under the underweight, normal weight, overweight, or obesity range.

BMI does not directly measure body fat, but it gives a quick general idea of body weight status. Many people use BMI as a first-level health screening calculation before checking other fitness and medical factors.

What is BMI?

BMI stands for Body Mass Index. It compares a person’s body weight with their height. The result is a numerical value that can be matched with BMI categories.

For example, two people may have the same weight, but their BMI can be different if their heights are different. That is why BMI uses both height and weight instead of weight alone.

BMI Formula

The standard BMI formula uses weight in kilograms and height in meters.

BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ Height² (m²)

If your height is in centimeters, first convert it into meters. For example, 170 cm is equal to 1.70 meters.

How to Calculate BMI?

To calculate BMI manually, follow these simple steps:

  1. Measure your weight in kilograms.
  2. Measure your height in centimeters.
  3. Convert height from centimeters to meters.
  4. Multiply height in meters by itself.
  5. Divide your weight by the squared height value.

BMI Calculation Example

Let us calculate BMI for a person with a weight of 70 kg and height of 175 cm.

Height = 175 cm = 1.75 m
BMI = 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 22.86

So, the BMI is 22.86. This falls under the normal weight range for most adults.

How to Calculate BMI in kg and cm?

Most people know their height in centimeters and weight in kilograms. In that case, convert height from centimeters to meters before using the formula.

Height in meters = Height in cm ÷ 100

For example, if your height is 160 cm, your height in meters is 1.60 m. Then square the height and divide your weight by that value.

How to Calculate BMI in Pounds and Inches?

If you use pounds and inches, the BMI formula is slightly different.

BMI = (Weight in pounds ÷ Height in inches²) × 703

For example, if weight is 154 lb and height is 69 inches, BMI is calculated by dividing 154 by 69 squared and multiplying by 703.

Why is BMI Used?

BMI is widely used because it is easy, quick, and does not require special equipment. It helps users get a basic understanding of weight category using only height and weight.

  • To check general body weight category.
  • To understand whether weight is low, normal, or high for height.
  • To track weight changes over time.
  • To support fitness and wellness planning.
  • To start a basic health discussion with a professional.

Limitations of BMI

BMI is useful, but it does not tell the full story about health. It does not measure body fat percentage, muscle mass, bone density, body shape, or fat distribution.

For example, an athlete with high muscle mass may have a high BMI but low body fat. Similarly, BMI may not be suitable as a single measure for children, pregnant women, elderly people, and people with specific health conditions.

BMI and Healthy Weight

A normal BMI range is often used as a general healthy weight indicator for adults. However, health also depends on diet, activity level, waist size, sleep, medical history, and other factors.

If your BMI is very low or very high, it is better to discuss it with a qualified health professional instead of depending only on an online result.

Common BMI Mistakes to Avoid

  • Do not use centimeters directly in the meter-based BMI formula.
  • Do not forget to square the height value.
  • Do not treat BMI as a complete health diagnosis.
  • Do not compare adult BMI directly with child BMI.
  • Do not ignore muscle mass, age, and body composition.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the formula to calculate BMI?

The BMI formula is BMI = weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared. In short, BMI = kg / m².

2. How do I calculate BMI in kg and cm?

First convert height from centimeters to meters. Then divide weight in kilograms by height in meters squared.

3. What is a normal BMI range?

For most adults, a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is commonly considered the normal weight range.

4. Is BMI accurate for everyone?

BMI is a useful screening tool, but it may not be accurate for athletes, pregnant women, children, older adults, or people with high muscle mass.

5. Can BMI tell body fat percentage?

No, BMI does not directly measure body fat percentage. It only compares height and weight to estimate general weight category.
