How to Calculate Net Run Rate in Cricket: Formula, Examples & IPL Rules

Net run rate is one of the most important numbers on a cricket points table. This guide explains the formula, how to read overs and balls, what happens when a team is all out, and why a tournament's NRR differs from the result of a single match.

What is net run rate (NRR)?

Net run rate (NRR) is the difference between a team's average scoring rate and the average rate at which it concedes runs in the matches that count for the table. It is expressed in runs per over. If a team scores at 9 runs per over while conceding 8 runs per over, its NRR for that match is +1.000.

Leagues such as the IPL use NRR as one of their tie-breaking measures when teams finish level on points and wins. A positive NRR generally reflects more efficient scoring relative to opponents, but it does not directly indicate how many matches a team has won. Competition-specific ranking and tie-breaking rules still apply.

Net run rate formula in cricket

NRR = (Total runs scored ÷ Total overs faced) − (Total runs conceded ÷ Total overs bowled)

The formula looks straightforward, but “total” matters. For a season or group stage, add the runs and overs from every match that counts, then divide once for the batting rate and once for the bowling rate. Do not simply average the NRR figures from individual matches.

What the four values mean

  • Total runs scored: all runs made by your team across the counted innings.
  • Total overs faced: the actual legal balls your team faced, converted to overs, except for innings declared all out under the full-overs rule.
  • Total runs conceded: all runs made by opponents against your team in those counted matches.
  • Total overs bowled: the effective overs used by opponents, with the same all-out adjustment where applicable.

Example 1: calculate NRR for one T20 match

Imagine Team A scores 180 runs in 20 overs. Team B replies with 160 runs in 20 overs. No side is dismissed early and there is no DLS revision.

Team A scoring rate = 180 ÷ 20 = 9.000
Team A conceding rate = 160 ÷ 20 = 8.000
Net run rate = 9.000 − 8.000 = +1.000

Team A's net run rate contribution in this example is positive because it made one more run per over than it conceded. Team B's corresponding match NRR is −1.000.

Example 2: how to convert 18.4 overs correctly

Cricket scorecards use the digit after the decimal point to show legal balls, not decimal fractions of an over. Six valid balls make one full over, so the number after the point may only be 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5.

Example 3: how all-out innings affect NRR

Suppose Team A is dismissed for 140 after 18.4 overs of a scheduled 20-over innings. Under IPL net run rate rules, Team A is treated as having used the full 20 overs because it was all out. Its credited scoring rate is therefore 140 ÷ 20 = 7.000, not 140 ÷ 18.6667.

If the opposition makes 160 in 20 overs, Team A's match NRR calculation is (140 ÷ 20) − (160 ÷ 20) = −1.000. The team being dismissed early must not be rewarded with a higher rate simply because fewer balls were faced.

Important: Do not mark an innings all out simply because it ended before the full quota. A successful run chase can end early without the team being all out; in that case, the actual legal balls faced are used.

How IPL NRR rules work

In the published IPL 2026 playing conditions, a team's NRR is based on scoring and conceding averages over the season. For an all-out innings, the full entitled quota is used rather than the overs actually consumed. Matches with results are counted; rain-affected matches determined using Duckworth–Lewis–Stern (DLS) may need the credited runs and overs set by the playing conditions.

Which matches should you include?

  • Ordinary completed matches: include the match's runs and overs.
  • All-out innings: use the full quota the batting side was entitled to.
  • No-result or abandoned games: do not add them when the tournament does not record a result.
  • DLS matches with a result: use the competition's special NRR crediting figures; do not assume raw scoreboard totals are correct for NRR.
  • Super Overs: do not add extra one-over tie-break scores to the normal-innings NRR inputs.

For the official wording, consult the IPL 2026 Match Playing Conditions, clause 16.12. Specific tournament regulations may differ and should always be checked before calculating official standings.

Why is net run rate important for qualification?

In a close league table, teams can finish with the same points. Depending on the competition rules, wins, net run rate and further tie-breakers may determine their positions. A heavy defeat can significantly hurt NRR, and a quick chase can improve the scoring-rate component when combined with season totals. However, qualifying scenarios require the entire points table, remaining matches and the applicable tie-break rules; an NRR figure alone cannot guarantee a playoff position.

Common NRR mistakes to avoid

  1. Reading 18.4 as 18.4 decimal overs: convert it to 18 + 4/6 first.
  2. Ignoring all-out innings: use the full entitled quota for an all-out innings.
  3. Averaging match NRRs: combine runs and overs across the competition before dividing.
  4. Adding abandoned games: only matches with a result count under the applicable rules.
  5. Using raw rain-adjusted scores: DLS NRR needs special credited values; check the official event table.
  6. Including Super Over scores: keep the one-over tie-break separate from the main match's run-rate figures.

Frequently asked questions

What is NRR full form in cricket?

NRR stands for Net Run Rate. It compares a team's scoring rate against the rate at which opponents score against it.

How do I calculate net run rate in T20 cricket?

Divide runs scored by effective overs faced and subtract runs conceded divided by effective overs bowled, using six-ball overs and the full quota for any all-out innings.

What does +1.5 NRR mean?

It means that, across the counted matches, the team's average scoring rate exceeds the rate at which it concedes runs by 1.5 runs per over. It is not an extra 1.5 points in the league table.

Can a team have a negative NRR after winning a match?

Yes. Tournament NRR uses all counted matches. Earlier heavy defeats can leave the overall figure below zero even after a win.

How do I calculate net run rate after a rain delay?

Use the DLS-adjusted runs and overs credited under the tournament's playing conditions rather than directly using the unadjusted scoreboard numbers. This is not calculated automatically by CalcuPortal's basic tool.

Which is better: +0.5 or −0.5 net run rate?

All else being equal, +0.5 is higher than −0.5. But competition rankings can use points, wins and other tie-breakers before or after NRR.
