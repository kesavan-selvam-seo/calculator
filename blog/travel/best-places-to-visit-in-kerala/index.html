Best Places to Visit in Kerala

Explore the best places to visit in Kerala, including hill stations, backwaters, beaches, wildlife areas and heritage destinations.

Kerala offers very different travel experiences within one state: tea-covered hills around Munnar, forests and wildlife in Thekkady and Wayanad, backwaters around Alappuzha and Kumarakom, historic streets in Kochi, and beaches such as Varkala and Kovalam. Kerala Tourism also groups the state's attractions across hills, backwaters, beaches, culture and nature, so a good itinerary should be built around regions rather than a long checklist of places.

This guide covers 25+ places to visit in Kerala and helps you choose destinations based on your trip length, interests and preferred pace.

Best Hill Stations in Kerala

1. Munnar

Munnar is one of Kerala's best-known hill destinations, with extensive tea plantations, misty hills, waterfalls and viewpoints. Kerala Tourism lists attractions such as Eravikulam National Park, Top Station, Mattupetty Dam, Kundala Lake and the Tata Tea Museum around the Munnar region. It works well for families, couples and travellers looking for scenic mountain landscapes.

2. Wayanad

Wayanad combines forests, wildlife, waterfalls and heritage attractions. Kerala Tourism highlights places such as Edakkal Caves, Chembra Peak and Banasura Sagar Dam. It is a good choice when you want a hill destination with more nature and outdoor variety rather than a tea-estate-focused experience.

3. Thekkady

Thekkady is best known for the Periyar landscape and wildlife experiences. Travellers can combine the area with boat-based activities, forest experiences and spice-related visits. It works particularly well as part of a Munnar–Thekkady route.

4. Vagamon

Vagamon offers rolling meadows, pine forests and a quieter hill-station atmosphere. It can suit travellers who want a relaxed mountain break or outdoor activities without choosing one of Kerala's busiest hill destinations.

5. Ponmudi

Ponmudi is a hill destination near Thiruvananthapuram with winding roads, misty landscapes and viewpoints. It is useful for travellers combining a southern Kerala city trip with a short mountain escape.

Best Backwater Destinations in Kerala

6. Alappuzha (Alleppey)

Alappuzha is one of the most recognisable backwater destinations in Kerala. Houseboat cruises, canals, paddy fields and village landscapes make it a natural choice for travellers who want a classic backwater experience. A cruise can be planned as a day activity or an overnight stay depending on the itinerary.

7. Kumarakom

Kumarakom sits on the Vembanad Lake backwater system and is known for relaxed waterfront stays and nature experiences. It works well for travellers who prefer a slower backwater holiday rather than a tightly scheduled sightseeing route.

8. Kollam

Kollam offers another route into Kerala's backwater landscape, with lakes, waterways and coastal attractions. It can be useful for travellers moving between central and southern Kerala rather than making a separate long detour.

Best Beaches in Kerala

9. Varkala

Varkala combines a dramatic cliffside setting with a beach below and a strong coastal holiday atmosphere. It is a practical choice for travellers who want beach time, cafes and a relaxed stay in southern Kerala.

10. Kovalam

Kovalam is a long-established beach destination near Thiruvananthapuram. It works well for travellers combining a beach stay with city sightseeing and other southern Kerala attractions.

11. Marari Beach

Marari is a quieter beach option near Alappuzha. It suits travellers who want to add a few relaxed beach days to a central Kerala backwater itinerary.

12. Bekal

Bekal is known for its fort and coastal setting in Kasaragod. It is especially useful for travellers exploring North Kerala or combining beaches with heritage attractions.

13. Muzhappilangad Beach

Muzhappilangad is a notable Kannur-area beach and can be included in a North Kerala itinerary along with other Kannur attractions.

Wildlife and Nature Destinations

14. Periyar Tiger Reserve

The Periyar landscape around Thekkady is a major choice for wildlife-focused travel. Boat-based and other permitted nature experiences can be combined with a stay in Thekkady. Check current park rules and activity availability before booking.

15. Eravikulam National Park

Eravikulam National Park is one of the major attractions around Munnar and is known for its Nilgiri tahr habitat and mountain scenery. Access, opening schedules and seasonal restrictions can change, so verify current information before visiting.

16. Silent Valley National Park

Silent Valley is a strong option for travellers interested in forest landscapes and biodiversity. It is better suited to a dedicated nature itinerary than a quick stop between distant cities.

17. Gavi

Gavi offers a forest-focused experience in Pathanamthitta district. Travellers should check current access arrangements and permitted activities because protected-area visits can have specific rules.

18. Athirappilly Falls

Athirappilly is one of Kerala's most recognisable waterfall destinations and can be combined with nearby nature attractions. It works particularly well as a day trip from central Kerala when road conditions and time allow.

Heritage, Culture and City Attractions

19. Kochi and Fort Kochi

Kochi is a strong choice for travellers who want history, food, architecture and a city experience alongside Kerala's natural attractions. Fort Kochi and Mattancherry add heritage-focused sightseeing, while the city also works as a convenient starting point for a wider Kerala itinerary.

20. Mattancherry Palace

Mattancherry is part of Kochi's heritage circuit and can be grouped with Fort Kochi and nearby cultural attractions. Allow enough time to explore the area rather than treating each heritage site as an isolated stop.

21. Guruvayur

Guruvayur is an important pilgrimage destination and is often included in cultural and temple-focused Kerala trips. Visitors should check current temple entry rules and timings before planning a visit.

22. Thiruvananthapuram

Kerala's capital offers a mix of heritage, culture, museums and access to nearby coastal destinations. It works well as a base when combining Kovalam, Varkala and southern Kerala attractions.

23. Kozhikode

Kozhikode is a strong North Kerala stop for travellers interested in coastal culture, food and history. It can be combined with nearby attractions and used as a base for exploring the Malabar region.

More Places to Visit in North Kerala

24. Kannur

Kannur offers beaches, cultural experiences and heritage attractions. It is also useful as a base for exploring the northern coast without trying to combine it with far-away central Kerala destinations on the same day.

25. Kappad Beach

Kappad is a coastal destination near Kozhikode with historical associations and a relaxed beach setting. It fits naturally into a Kozhikode-area itinerary.

26. Malampuzha

Malampuzha is a popular Palakkad-area destination with gardens, a dam and surrounding hill landscapes. It is a practical addition to a Palakkad or central Kerala road trip.

27. Vagamon and nearby Idukki

Travellers with additional time can explore more of Idukki beyond Munnar and Thekkady. Vagamon and nearby hill areas provide a slower alternative to the more established tourist circuits.

Kerala Itinerary by Number of Days

3-Day Kerala Trip

Keep a three-day trip focused. Kochi plus either Munnar or Alappuzha can work better than trying to cover hills, backwaters and beaches across the whole state.

5-Day Kerala Trip

A five-day itinerary can combine Kochi, Munnar and Alappuzha, with Thekkady added only if travel times fit comfortably. Keep the number of hotel changes limited.

7-Day Kerala Trip

Seven days allow a more balanced first trip. A common structure is Kochi → Munnar → Thekkady → Alappuzha, followed by either a return to Kochi or a southern coastal extension depending on transport plans.

10-Day Kerala Trip

Ten days give you room to add Varkala or Kovalam, explore more of North Kerala, or slow down the hill and backwater portions. The best route depends on your arrival and departure airports.

Practical Tips for Visiting Kerala

  1. Plan by region: Kerala is geographically spread out, so avoid crossing from far north to far south for a single attraction.
  2. Check weather: Rain can affect hill roads, waterfalls, beaches and outdoor activities.
  3. Book popular stays early: Houseboats and popular hill destinations can be busy during holidays and peak travel periods.
  4. Check official timings: National parks, temples, museums and heritage sites can have different schedules and rules.
  5. Allow transfer time: A route that looks short on a map can take longer because of winding hill roads and local traffic.
  6. Choose the right base: Staying near the attractions you want to visit can reduce daily travel.
  7. Keep nature visits flexible: Wildlife and protected-area access can depend on weather, season and current regulations.
  8. Do not overpack: Two or three well-planned destinations can provide a better trip than a long checklist.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many days are enough to visit Kerala?

A 5–7 day trip works well for a first visit covering a few major regions, while 8–10 days allow a slower itinerary with more destinations.

Which are the best places to visit in Kerala for first-time visitors?

Munnar, Thekkady, Alappuzha, Kochi and Varkala are common choices for a first trip because they combine hills, wildlife, backwaters, heritage and beaches.

Which is better, Munnar or Wayanad?

Munnar is known for tea plantations, mountain scenery and attractions such as Eravikulam National Park, while Wayanad combines forests, wildlife, waterfalls and heritage sites. The choice depends on your interests and route.

What are the best hill stations in Kerala?

Munnar, Wayanad, Thekkady, Vagamon and Ponmudi are among the hill destinations highlighted by Kerala Tourism.

Which are the best backwater destinations in Kerala?

Alappuzha, Kumarakom and Kollam are popular choices for backwater experiences, including houseboat and water-based sightseeing.

What are the best beaches to visit in Kerala?

Varkala, Kovalam, Marari, Bekal and Muzhappilangad are popular beach destinations, with different landscapes and activities.

What are the best places to visit in Kerala with family?

Families can consider Munnar, Thekkady, Alappuzha, Kochi, Kumarakom and selected beaches depending on the group's pace and interests.

What are the best places to visit in Kerala for couples?

Munnar, Kumarakom, Varkala and Vagamon are popular choices for couples looking for scenic, relaxed or nature-focused stays.

What is the best month to visit Kerala?

The best month depends on the experience you want. Weather, monsoon conditions and regional activities vary, so check the forecast and destination-specific conditions before booking.

How should I plan a Kerala trip itinerary?

Group destinations by region instead of crossing the state repeatedly. A common first-trip structure is Kochi, Munnar, Thekkady and Alappuzha, with Varkala or another coastal destination added when time permits.
