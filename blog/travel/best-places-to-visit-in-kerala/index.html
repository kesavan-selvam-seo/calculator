Best Places to Visit in Kerala

Explore Kerala's hill stations, backwaters, beaches, wildlife destinations and heritage attractions, with practical ideas for planning a 3-day, 5-day or 7-day trip.

The best places to visit in Kerala depend on the kind of trip you want to take. The state combines tea-covered hills, wildlife reserves, palm-fringed backwaters, beaches, historic towns and cultural attractions. Munnar, Wayanad, Thekkady, Alappuzha, Kochi, Varkala and Kovalam are among the destinations commonly included in Kerala travel plans, but a good itinerary should be built around geography rather than a long checklist.

Best Hill Stations in Kerala

Kerala's Western Ghats make the state especially attractive for hill-station trips. Munnar, Wayanad, Thekkady, Vagamon and Ponmudi offer different experiences, from tea estates and wildlife to meadows and winding mountain roads. Kerala Tourism specifically highlights these destinations among its hill stations.

1. Munnar

Munnar is one of Kerala's most popular hill destinations, known for tea plantations, misty hills, valleys and cool-weather sightseeing. The wider Munnar region includes attractions such as Eravikulam National Park, Mattupetty Dam, Kundala Lake, Top Station and the Tata Tea Museum. Kerala Tourism places Munnar in Idukki district at around 1,600 metres above sea level.

2. Wayanad

Wayanad is a strong choice if you want forests, wildlife and outdoor activities rather than a conventional hill-station town. Edakkal Caves, Banasura Sagar Dam, Chembra Peak and Wayanad Wildlife Sanctuary are among the attractions associated with the region.

3. Thekkady

Thekkady is best known as the gateway to the Periyar landscape and its wildlife experiences. Visitors can combine the destination with Periyar Lake, nature activities and spice plantations. Kerala Tourism identifies Periyar Tiger Reserve in Thekkady as one of Kerala's major wildlife destinations.

4. Vagamon

Vagamon offers rolling meadows, pine-covered landscapes and a quieter hill-station experience. It can suit travellers looking for scenic drives, outdoor activities and a less urban holiday atmosphere. Kerala Tourism highlights Vagamon for its meadows, waterfalls and adventure options.

5. Ponmudi

Ponmudi is a hill destination in Thiruvananthapuram district known for misty mountain scenery, winding roads, tea estates and waterfalls. It is particularly practical as a hill getaway when your wider trip is based around southern Kerala.

Best Backwater Destinations in Kerala

Backwaters are one of Kerala's defining travel experiences. Instead of treating every backwater destination as interchangeable, choose between Alappuzha for the classic houseboat experience, Kumarakom for a quieter Vembanad Lake stay, or Kollam for a different southern backwater landscape.

6. Alappuzha (Alleppey)

Alappuzha is one of the best-known backwater destinations in Kerala. Kerala Tourism describes it as a renowned backwater destination and highlights the Alappuzha backwaters, Kuttanad and Alappuzha Beach. A houseboat trip is the classic way to experience the waterways, but shorter canoe and village experiences can also work well for travellers with limited time.

7. Kumarakom

Kumarakom sits on the Vembanad Lake and is suited to travellers who want a slower backwater holiday. It works particularly well as part of a relaxed itinerary with lakeside stays, birdwatching and nearby village experiences.

8. Kollam

Kollam provides another gateway to Kerala's waterways and coastal landscapes. The wider district includes Ashtamudi Backwaters, Thenmala, Palaruvi and several beaches, making it useful for travellers who want to combine backwaters with nature and coastal sightseeing.

Best Beaches to Visit in Kerala

Kerala's coastline includes both popular beach destinations and quieter stretches. Varkala and Kovalam are convenient choices for many first-time visitors, while Bekal and Muzhappilangad can add a different coastal experience to a northern Kerala itinerary.

9. Varkala Beach

Varkala Beach is distinctive for its laterite cliff setting and is a natural choice for travellers who want a beach combined with cafés, viewpoints and nearby cultural attractions. Kerala Tourism also highlights the mineral springs, Sivagiri Mutt and Janardhanaswamy Temple in the wider Varkala area.

10. Kovalam

Kovalam is one of Kerala's established beach destinations near Thiruvananthapuram. Kerala Tourism describes its three adjacent crescent-shaped beaches and notes activities including swimming, cruising and wellness experiences.

11. Bekal

Bekal is ideal for travellers who want a combination of a historic fort and coastal scenery. Bekal Fort stands close to the Arabian Sea and can be combined with northern Kerala sightseeing rather than treated as a quick stop from southern destinations.

12. Muzhappilangad Beach

Muzhappilangad Beach near Kannur is notable for its drive-in beach setting. Kerala Tourism identifies it as a drive-in beach stretching for about 4 km, making it a distinctive addition to a North Kerala road trip.

Wildlife and Nature Destinations in Kerala

13. Periyar Tiger Reserve

Periyar Tiger Reserve in Thekkady is one of Kerala's major wildlife destinations. The reserve is associated with Periyar Lake, forest landscapes and wildlife-viewing experiences. Access, activities and timings should be checked through current official information before booking.

14. Eravikulam National Park

Eravikulam National Park is near Munnar and is known for its mountain ecosystem and the endangered Nilgiri tahr. It is one of the attractions that makes Munnar more than a tea-estate destination.

15. Silent Valley National Park

Silent Valley National Park is a major nature destination in the Western Ghats and suits travellers who want forests and biodiversity rather than conventional sightseeing. It needs more deliberate planning than a city attraction, so check access arrangements before travelling.

16. Gavi

Gavi in Pathanamthitta is an eco-tourism destination associated with trekking, wildlife watching, camping and forest experiences. It is better suited to a nature-focused itinerary than a quick stop between major cities.

17. Athirappilly Waterfalls

Athirappilly is one of Kerala's well-known waterfall destinations. It can work as a dedicated day trip or as part of a Thrissur–Kochi route, depending on your starting point and travel time. Kerala Tourism includes Athirappilly among the state's major destinations and waterfall attractions.

Heritage and Cultural Places in Kerala

18. Fort Kochi

Fort Kochi is a strong choice for travellers who want architecture, history, food and cultural experiences in one compact area. Kerala Tourism describes Fort Kochi as a historic enclave with diverse attractions, architecture and cuisine.

19. Mattancherry

Mattancherry adds another layer to a Kochi itinerary, particularly for travellers interested in historic streets, cultural landmarks and the area's trading history. It pairs naturally with Fort Kochi rather than requiring a separate day.

20. Guruvayur

Guruvayur is one of Kerala's major pilgrimage destinations. It is most relevant to travellers interested in temples and religious tourism and can be combined with Thrissur-region sightseeing.

21. Bekal Fort

Bekal Fort combines history and coastal views and is one of the signature attractions of Kasaragod. It is particularly useful as an anchor for a North Kerala itinerary.

Places to Visit in North Kerala

North Kerala is often overlooked by first-time visitors who concentrate on Kochi, Munnar and Alappuzha. If you have more time, destinations around Kannur, Kozhikode and Kasaragod offer beaches, food, forts and cultural experiences with a distinctly Malabar character.

22. Kannur

Kannur is a practical base for exploring northern beaches, forts and cultural attractions. It works well for travellers building a coastal North Kerala road trip.

23. Kozhikode

Kozhikode combines beaches, food and Malabar culture. It can be a useful stop between central Kerala and the northern districts, particularly if you want to experience the region's local cuisine.

24. Kappad Beach

Kappad Beach near Kozhikode is a quieter coastal option with historical associations. It fits naturally into a Kozhikode-area itinerary rather than a southern Kerala circuit.

25. Malampuzha

Malampuzha near Palakkad is a practical addition if your journey includes the Palakkad region. It provides a change from Kerala's coastal and backwater destinations and can be paired with other Palakkad-area attractions.

North Kerala vs South Kerala

There is no single itinerary that works for everyone. North Kerala is useful when you want Malabar culture, beaches and northern heritage, while central and southern Kerala make it easier to combine destinations such as Kochi, Munnar, Alappuzha, Varkala and Kovalam.

How Many Days Do You Need to Visit Kerala?

3 days in Kerala

Three days are best treated as a focused regional trip. A practical option is Kochi plus Munnar, or Alappuzha plus nearby destinations. Avoid trying to cover Munnar, Thekkady, Alleppey and Varkala in three days.

5 days in Kerala

Five days allow a more balanced route. You could combine Kochi, Munnar and Alappuzha, or build a southern itinerary around Munnar, Thekkady and a backwater destination depending on your arrival and departure points.

7 days in Kerala

A week gives you more room to combine hills, wildlife, backwaters and the coast. You can also slow down the itinerary instead of changing hotels every night.

10 days or more

With ten days or longer, you can consider adding Wayanad, North Kerala or a longer coastal stay. The extra time is particularly useful because Kerala's destinations are spread across a long state with varied road journeys.

Practical Tips for Visiting Kerala

  • Choose a region first: Kerala is geographically spread out, so do not build an itinerary by simply picking the most searched destinations.
  • Group nearby places: This reduces hotel changes and unnecessary road travel.
  • Allow time for hills: Mountain roads can take longer than the distance suggests.
  • Check attraction timings: National parks, museums, temples, waterfalls and other attractions may have specific access rules.
  • Keep monsoon plans flexible: Rain can affect outdoor activities and road travel.
  • Book popular stays early: Availability can change during holidays and peak travel periods.
  • Use realistic transfer times: Do not schedule a major attraction immediately after a long intercity journey.
  • Respect local rules: Follow instructions at wildlife areas, religious sites, heritage monuments and protected landscapes.
  • Check official information: Access, timings and operating arrangements can change, so verify them close to your travel date.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best places to visit in Kerala for a first trip?

Munnar, Kochi and Alappuzha make a practical combination because they offer different experiences: hill scenery, heritage and backwaters. If you have more time, add Thekkady or Varkala.

Which are the best hill stations in Kerala?

Munnar, Wayanad, Thekkady, Vagamon and Ponmudi are among Kerala's well-known hill destinations, with different combinations of tea plantations, forests, wildlife, meadows and mountain scenery.

Which places are best for backwaters in Kerala?

Alappuzha, Kumarakom and Kollam are useful choices. Alappuzha is especially associated with houseboat and backwater experiences, while Kumarakom offers a quieter Vembanad Lake setting.

Which beaches are worth visiting in Kerala?

Varkala, Kovalam, Bekal and Muzhappilangad are among the state's notable beach destinations. Choose based on your route and whether you want cliffs, a resort-style beach stay, a fort or a distinctive coastal experience.

How many days are enough to visit Kerala?

Three days can work for one region, five days allow a broader route, and seven days give you more flexibility to combine hills, wildlife, backwaters and the coast.

What are the best places to visit in Kerala for families?

Munnar, Kumarakom, Alappuzha and Thekkady can work well for families because they offer a mix of scenery, relaxed stays and different sightseeing options. Choose activities based on the ages and interests of your group.

What are the best places to visit in Kerala for couples?

Munnar, Varkala and Kumarakom are popular choices for couples looking for scenic surroundings, relaxed stays, hills, beaches or backwater experiences.

Is Wayanad worth visiting on a Kerala trip?

Wayanad is a strong option if you enjoy forests, wildlife, viewpoints, caves and outdoor activities. It is better added as part of a North Kerala or dedicated hill itinerary than squeezed into a short southern circuit.

Is Munnar or Wayanad better for a Kerala trip?

They offer different experiences. Munnar is especially known for tea plantations and mountain scenery, while Wayanad combines forests, wildlife, caves and viewpoints. Choose based on the type of trip you want and your route.

What is the best way to plan a Kerala trip?

Choose two or three destination clusters based on your trip length, group nearby places, allow realistic road time and check current attraction timings and access rules before departure.
