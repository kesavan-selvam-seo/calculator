Important Lakes in India

Explore India's major freshwater lakes, salt lakes, lagoons and high-altitude lakes with their states, types and key geography facts.

India has many important lakes and wetland systems ranging from Himalayan freshwater lakes and high-altitude lakes to coastal lagoons, saline lakes and reservoirs. Wular, Chilika, Sambhar, Loktak and Vembanad are among the most frequently discussed in Indian geography and general knowledge.

This guide covers the important lakes in India by state, type and geographical significance, making it useful for school students, GK quizzes and competitive-exam revision. The focus is on facts that are commonly asked, while avoiding claims that depend on changing water levels or seasonal lake area.

Important Freshwater Lakes in India

1. Wular Lake – Jammu and Kashmir

Wular Lake is a major freshwater lake in the Kashmir Valley, located in the Bandipora area and connected with the Jhelum river system. Government sources describe it as India's largest freshwater lake and a Ramsar site. Its wetland ecosystem supports fish, aquatic vegetation and resident and migratory birds.

Wular is also important geographically because it acts as a natural flood-absorption basin for the Kashmir Valley. For GK, remember: Wular → Jammu and Kashmir → freshwater → Jhelum basin → Ramsar site.

2. Dal Lake – Jammu and Kashmir

Dal Lake is one of the best-known lakes of Srinagar. It is associated with houseboats, shikaras, floating gardens and tourism. Its cultural and economic importance makes it a common geography and tourism-related GK topic.

3. Loktak Lake – Manipur

Loktak Lake is the largest freshwater lake in Northeast India. It is famous for phumdis, floating masses made up of vegetation, soil and organic matter. The southern part of the lake includes Keibul Lamjao National Park, known as the world's only floating national park and the natural habitat of the Sangai deer. Loktak was designated a Ramsar wetland in 1990.

4. Kolleru Lake – Andhra Pradesh

Kolleru Lake is a major freshwater lake and wetland between the Krishna and Godavari river systems in Andhra Pradesh. It is internationally recognised as a Ramsar wetland and is important for fish, wetland vegetation and migratory birds. The lake and Kolleru Wildlife Sanctuary are closely associated with bird conservation.

5. Vembanad Lake – Kerala

Vembanad Lake is the largest lake in Kerala and a central part of the state's backwater network. Kerala Tourism describes it as a major backwater destination spread across parts of Alappuzha and Kottayam districts, with rivers and canals connecting to the lake. It is associated with fishing, inland transport, tourism and the famous snake boat races.

6. Nainital Lake – Uttarakhand

Nainital Lake is a natural freshwater lake around which the hill town of Nainital developed. It is surrounded by the Kumaon Himalayas and is important for tourism and the local landscape.

Saltwater and Brackish Lakes in India

Chilika Lake – Odisha

Chilika Lake is a large brackish-water lagoon on the Odisha coast. Odisha Tourism describes it as Asia's largest brackish-water lagoon and highlights its importance for migratory birds, biodiversity and Irrawaddy dolphins. Chilika is also a Ramsar site and a wetland of international importance.

For exams, remember: Chilika → Odisha → brackish water → lagoon → migratory birds → Ramsar site.

Sambhar Lake – Rajasthan

Sambhar Lake is India's largest inland salt lake and one of the country's most important saline wetlands. It lies in Rajasthan and is known for salt production as well as its importance for waterbirds. Government sources identify it as a Ramsar site and note that its water level and depth vary considerably with the season.

Pulicat Lake – Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu

Pulicat Lake is a brackish-water lagoon extending across Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu. The Tamil Nadu Wildlife Division identifies it as an important coastal lagoon and a habitat for migratory birds. Its location along the east coast makes it useful for questions on Indian lagoons and wetlands.

Easy memory trick: Chilika and Pulicat are coastal brackish lagoons, while Sambhar is an inland saline lake. Wular and Loktak are important freshwater lakes.

Important High-Altitude Lakes in India

Pangong Tso – Ladakh

Pangong Tso is a high-altitude lake in the Ladakh region extending across the India–China border. Its long, narrow basin, high elevation and changing colours make it one of the best-known lakes in the Indian Himalayas. Because it is a transboundary water body, its geographical location should be described as extending beyond India rather than as a lake wholly within India.

Tso Moriri – Ladakh

Tso Moriri is a high-altitude lake in the Changthang region of Ladakh. It forms part of a fragile cold-desert wetland ecosystem and is important for birds and other wildlife. It is also recognised as a Ramsar site.

Tsomgo Lake – Sikkim

Tsomgo Lake, also called Changu Lake, is a high-altitude glacial lake in Sikkim. It is located close to the India–China border and is an important natural and tourism landmark of the state.

Other Important Lakes in India

Lonar Lake – Maharashtra

Lonar Lake is a distinctive saline and alkaline crater lake in Buldhana district, Maharashtra. The crater was created by a meteor impact in basaltic rock, making Lonar an important geo-heritage site as well as a lake of scientific interest.

Ashtamudi Lake – Kerala

Ashtamudi Lake is a major backwater wetland in Kerala and a Ramsar site. Its extensive network of waterways is important for fisheries, local livelihoods, transport and tourism.

Sasthamkotta Lake – Kerala

Sasthamkotta Lake is an important freshwater lake in Kerala and is recognised as a Ramsar wetland. It is significant for freshwater resources and the surrounding ecosystem.

Deepor Beel – Assam

Deepor Beel is a major freshwater wetland near Guwahati in Assam. It is an important habitat for waterbirds and is listed as a Ramsar site. The wetland also plays an important role in the ecology of the Brahmaputra valley.

Rudrasagar Lake – Tripura

Rudrasagar Lake is a freshwater wetland in Tripura, famous for its association with Neermahal palace. It is also a Ramsar site and supports wetland biodiversity.

Harike Lake – Punjab

Harike Lake is a major wetland and reservoir system in Punjab near the confluence of the Beas and Sutlej rivers. It is important for migratory and resident birds and is listed as a Ramsar site.

Hussain Sagar – Telangana

Hussain Sagar is an artificial lake in Hyderabad, historically important to the city's urban landscape. It is located between Hyderabad and Secunderabad and is one of Telangana's well-known water bodies.

Upper Lake (Bhojtal) – Madhya Pradesh

Upper Lake, also known as Bhojtal, is a historic artificial lake in Bhopal. It is an important part of the city's water landscape and is commonly included in Indian geography and general-awareness lists.

How to Study Important Lakes in India

For quick revision, avoid learning all lake names as one long list. Use a lake + state + type + one identifying fact pattern.

  1. Start with the high-frequency names: Wular, Chilika, Sambhar, Loktak and Vembanad.
  2. Group coastal lagoons together: Chilika and Pulicat.
  3. Group high-altitude lakes together: Pangong Tso, Tso Moriri and Tsomgo.
  4. Remember special formations separately: Lonar is the meteor-impact crater lake.
  5. Use state-based revision for lakes such as Vembanad and Ashtamudi in Kerala.
  6. For current Ramsar status or environmental details, verify the latest official wetland list because designations and site information can change.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is the largest freshwater lake in India?

Wular Lake in Jammu and Kashmir is commonly identified by Indian government sources as India's largest freshwater lake.

Which is the largest saltwater lake in India?

Sambhar Lake in Rajasthan is India's largest inland salt lake. It is a saline wetland and an important Ramsar site.

Which is the largest brackish-water lagoon in India?

Chilika Lake in Odisha is a large brackish-water lagoon and is widely described by Odisha Tourism as Asia's largest brackish-water lagoon.

Which lake is famous for floating islands?

Loktak Lake in Manipur is famous for floating phumdis, which are heterogeneous masses of vegetation, soil and organic matter.

Which is the largest lake in Kerala?

Vembanad Lake is the largest lake in Kerala and forms a major part of the state's backwater system.

Which Indian lake was formed by a meteor impact?

Lonar Lake in Maharashtra is a saline and alkaline crater lake formed by a meteor impact in basaltic rock.

Which lakes are important coastal lagoons in India?

Chilika in Odisha and Pulicat on the Andhra Pradesh–Tamil Nadu coast are two major brackish coastal lagoons.

Why are lakes important for India?

Lakes and wetlands support freshwater supply, fisheries, agriculture, biodiversity, flood regulation, tourism and the livelihoods of local communities.
