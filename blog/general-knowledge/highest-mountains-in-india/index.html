Highest Mountains and Peaks in India

India's mountain geography includes the Himalayas, Karakoram and several important ranges across the peninsula and northeast.

Kangchenjunga (8,586 m) is the highest mountain associated with India and the highest peak in the country. It is the world's third-highest mountain and lies on the India–Nepal border, with the Indian side in Sikkim. Other major high peaks in India include Nanda Devi, Kamet, Saser Kangri I and Mamostong Kangri.

This guide covers the highest mountains in India by elevation, their heights, states or regions, mountain ranges and the facts most useful for school GK, competitive exams, quizzes and geography revision.

1. Kangchenjunga – Highest Mountain Associated with India

Kangchenjunga, also spelled Kanchenjunga or Khangchendzonga, rises to 8,586 metres. It is the world's third-highest mountain after Mount Everest and K2. The peak lies on the India–Nepal border, with the Indian side in Sikkim.

Kangchenjunga is especially important in Indian geography because it is the country's highest peak and is deeply associated with Sikkim's landscape and cultural traditions. The Government of India's Press Information Bureau has described Kangchenjunga as India's highest and only 8,000-metre peak. citeturn0search10

For GK questions, remember three facts together: 8,586 m → Sikkim → third-highest mountain in the world. Darjeeling's Tiger Hill is also famous for views of Kangchenjunga. citeturn0search11

2. Nanda Devi – Second-Highest Peak in India

Nanda Devi rises to about 7,816–7,817 metres in Uttarakhand and is widely described by Indian government sources as India's second-highest peak. The Wildlife Institute of India identifies Nanda Devi West at 7,817 m within Nanda Devi National Park. citeturn0search0turn0search5

Nanda Devi is part of the Garhwal Himalayas and is closely linked with the Nanda Devi Biosphere Reserve and Nanda Devi National Park. The Nanda Devi and Valley of Flowers National Parks together form a UNESCO World Heritage property, recognised for their mountain landscapes and biodiversity. citeturn0search2

Exam fact: Nanda Devi is the highest peak located entirely within India in many standard GK references. Kangchenjunga is higher but lies on the India–Nepal border.

3. Mount Kamet – Third-Highest Major Peak

Mount Kamet reaches approximately 7,756 metres and is located in Uttarakhand's Garhwal Himalayas near the India–Tibet region. The Press Information Bureau has identified Kamet at 7,756 m and described it as India's third-highest peak after Kangchenjunga and Nanda Devi. citeturn0search3

Kamet is an important mountain in Indian mountaineering and geography. It is part of a high-altitude landscape containing several other major peaks and glaciers.

Why Are India's Highest Mountains Concentrated in the Himalayas?

India's tallest peaks are concentrated in the Himalayan and Karakoram mountain systems because these ranges contain extremely high elevations formed by the collision of tectonic plates and subsequent mountain-building processes.

The National Portal of India describes the Himalayan mountain zone as one of the country's major physical regions and notes that some of the world's highest peaks occur in these ranges. citeturn0search6

These mountains are important beyond their height. They influence river systems, climate, biodiversity, glaciers, tourism and livelihoods. The Himalayas also contain major watersheds connected to rivers that support large populations across South Asia.

How to Study Highest Mountains in India

For quick revision, memorise each major peak using a four-part pattern: name + height + state/region + range. For example, Nanda Devi → 7,816–7,817 m → Uttarakhand → Garhwal Himalayas.

  1. Learn the top three first: Kangchenjunga, Nanda Devi and Kamet.
  2. Group Ladakh peaks under the Karakoram and Uttarakhand peaks under the Garhwal/Kumaon Himalayas.
  3. Use a map to connect peaks with states and neighbouring regions.
  4. Do not mix up a mountain peak with a national park, mountain range or district.
  5. For current examination preparation, follow the latest official source where spellings or measurements differ.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is the highest mountain in India?

Kangchenjunga, at 8,586 metres, is India's highest mountain and the world's third-highest peak. It lies on the India–Nepal border, with the Indian side in Sikkim. citeturn0search10

Which is the highest mountain located entirely within India?

Nanda Devi, at about 7,816–7,817 metres, is generally identified as the highest peak located entirely within India.

What is the second-highest mountain in India?

Nanda Devi is commonly listed as India's second-highest peak when Kangchenjunga is included in India's rankings.

What is the third-highest mountain in India?

Mount Kamet, at about 7,756 metres, is commonly listed as India's third-highest peak. citeturn0search3

Which state has the highest mountain in India?

Kangchenjunga is associated with Sikkim on the Indian side, although the summit lies on the India–Nepal border.

How many mountains in India are above 7,000 metres?

There are numerous peaks above 7,000 metres across the Himalayas and Karakoram, but the exact count depends on how a source defines an independent peak and whether border peaks and subsidiary summits are included.
