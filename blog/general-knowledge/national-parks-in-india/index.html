National Parks in India

India's national parks protect diverse ecosystems ranging from Himalayan landscapes and tropical forests to grasslands, deserts, wetlands and marine habitats.

There are 106 existing national parks in India, covering about 44,403 sq km according to the Wildlife Institute of India's National Wildlife Database. National parks are important protected areas for conserving wildlife, plants and their habitats. For GK preparation, the most useful facts are the park's name, state or Union Territory, notable species and the ecosystem it protects.

How Many National Parks Are There in India?

As of the latest Wildlife Institute of India database information available for this guide, India has 106 existing national parks covering approximately 44,402.95 sq km. The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change also describes India's protected-area network as including national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, conservation reserves and community reserves.

The total number can change when new areas are notified, boundaries are modified or protected-area classifications are updated. Therefore, for an examination requiring a current count, it is better to verify the latest government database rather than relying on an old textbook.

Quick answer: India currently has 106 national parks. The Wildlife Institute of India lists them as protected areas notified for ecological, faunal, floral, geomorphological or zoological importance.

First National Park in India

Jim Corbett National Park is widely recognised as India's first national park. It was established in 1936 in the United Provinces and was originally known as Hailey National Park. It was later renamed in honour of Jim Corbett. The park is in present-day Uttarakhand and forms part of the larger Corbett Tiger Reserve landscape.

What Makes a National Park Different?

Under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972, a national park is an area notified for protecting its ecological, faunal, floral, geomorphological or zoological importance. The Wildlife Institute of India notes that national parks receive a high level of protection and that human activity is restricted except where specifically permitted under the law and by the competent authority.

Protected Area TypeBasic GK Difference
National ParkNotified primarily for strong protection of ecosystems and wildlife; activities are highly regulated.
Wildlife SanctuaryCreated for protection of wildlife and habitat, with legal provisions that can differ from national parks.
Conservation ReserveUsually government land, often serving as a conservation area or ecological corridor around protected habitats.
Community ReserveConservation area on community or private land where local participation has a formal role.

Important GK Facts About National Parks in India

  1. India currently has 106 existing national parks according to the Wildlife Institute of India's national-park database.
  2. India's first national park was established in 1936 and is now known as Jim Corbett National Park.
  3. Kaziranga is strongly associated with the one-horned rhinoceros.
  4. Gir is the best-known Indian national park for the Asiatic lion.
  5. Keibul Lamjao is associated with the floating wetland ecosystem of Loktak Lake and the Sangai deer.
  6. Hemis is a high-altitude national park in Ladakh and is associated with snow leopard habitat.
  7. Eravikulam is famous for the Nilgiri tahr and its high-altitude grassland landscape.
  8. Sundarbans protects part of the world's famous mangrove-dominated delta ecosystem and is associated with Bengal tiger habitat.
  9. Keoladeo is an important wetland and bird habitat in Rajasthan and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
  10. Great Himalayan and Valley of Flowers are among India's national parks recognised within UNESCO World Heritage properties.
  11. A national park can protect terrestrial, wetland, high-altitude, coastal or marine ecosystems.
  12. The number of national parks is a changeable administrative fact and should be checked against current government data for exams.

How to Prepare National Parks for GK

Do not try to memorise 106 names randomly. Build a simple three-layer map in your notes: Park → State/UT → Key species or ecosystem. Then revise the first national park and major UNESCO-associated parks separately.

  • Step 1: Learn the most frequently asked parks such as Corbett, Kaziranga, Gir, Kanha and Ranthambore.
  • Step 2: Add the state or Union Territory for each park.
  • Step 3: Attach one identifying species or ecosystem to each name.
  • Step 4: Group parks by Himalayas, Western Ghats, Northeast, central India, desert and coastal/marine regions.
  • Step 5: Recheck the current national-park count from an official source before an examination.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many national parks are there in India?

The Wildlife Institute of India currently lists 106 existing national parks in India.

Which was the first national park in India?

Jim Corbett National Park, established in 1936 and originally known as Hailey National Park, is recognised as India's first national park.

Which national park is famous for the one-horned rhinoceros?

Kaziranga National Park in Assam is especially famous for its one-horned rhinoceros population.

Which national park is famous for Asiatic lions?

Gir National Park in Gujarat is the best-known protected area associated with the Asiatic lion.

Which national park is associated with the Sangai deer?

Keibul Lamjao National Park in Manipur is associated with the Sangai deer and the floating wetland ecosystem of Loktak Lake.

Which national park is associated with the snow leopard?

Hemis National Park in Ladakh is a major high-altitude habitat associated with the snow leopard.

Which national park is famous for the Nilgiri tahr?

Eravikulam National Park in Kerala is strongly associated with the Nilgiri tahr.

Are national parks and wildlife sanctuaries the same?

No. Both are protected areas, but their legal provisions, permitted activities and management frameworks differ.

Can the number of national parks in India change?

Yes. New notifications, boundary changes and administrative updates can change the official count or details. Current government databases should be used for time-sensitive figures.

Where can I verify the current list of national parks in India?

The Wildlife Institute of India and the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change provide official protected-area information. State Forest Departments can also provide current park-specific notifications.
