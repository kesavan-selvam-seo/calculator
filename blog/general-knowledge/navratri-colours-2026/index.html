Navratri Colours 2026: 9 Days, Dates, Colours & Meanings

Updated October 4, 2026

Navratri colours 2026 for Sharad Navratri are orange, white, red, royal blue, yellow, green, grey, purple and peacock green. The colour calendar runs from 11 October to 19 October 2026, with Vijayadashami on 20 October. The day-wise colours are a popular festive custom; they are not a universal religious rule, and regional calendars may differ.

What Are the 9 Colours of Navratri?

The nine colours commonly listed for Sharad Navratri 2026 are orange, white, red, royal blue, yellow, green, grey, purple and peacock green. The sequence is not a permanent colour assignment for each Goddess. Instead, the annual colour calendar is a popular observance that changes with the weekday on which Navratri begins.

Why Do Navratri Colours Change Every Year?

The popular colour tradition is linked to the weekdays. Sunday is commonly associated with orange, Monday with white, Tuesday with red, Wednesday with royal blue, Thursday with yellow, Friday with green and Saturday with grey. The remaining two days use additional colours such as purple and peacock green. Because Navratri begins on a different weekday in different years, the day-wise sequence changes.

That is why a Navratri colour chart saved from 2025 should not automatically be reused for 2026. In 2026, the festival's first day falls on a Sunday, so the widely followed list starts with orange.

Navratri Colours 2026 and Their Meanings

The meanings below reflect common cultural and devotional associations. They should be understood as symbolic interpretations rather than guaranteed effects or universal scriptural rules.

Day 1 – Orange: Maa Shailaputri

Orange is commonly associated with warmth, enthusiasm, energy and a positive beginning. It gives the first day a bright festive tone.

Day 2 – White: Maa Brahmacharini

White is often associated with peace, purity, calmness and simplicity. It suits the devotional character commonly linked with Maa Brahmacharini.

Day 3 – Red: Maa Chandraghanta

Red is traditionally connected with strength, courage, passion and devotion. It is one of the most prominent festive colours used during Navratri.

Day 4 – Royal Blue: Maa Kushmanda

Royal blue is commonly associated with depth, stability, confidence and a sense of abundance. It adds a rich tone to the fourth day's celebrations.

Day 5 – Yellow: Maa Skandamata

Yellow is often linked with happiness, brightness, optimism and learning. It is a popular colour for festive clothing and decorations.

Day 6 – Green: Maa Katyayani

Green is associated with growth, renewal, harmony and freshness. It is also widely used in festive decorations and traditional outfits.

Day 7 – Grey: Maa Kalaratri

Grey is often interpreted as a balanced, grounded and composed shade. It offers a more understated alternative to the brighter colours used on other days.

Day 8 – Purple: Maa Mahagauri

Purple is commonly associated with dignity, richness and spiritual depth. It creates a distinct festive look for the eighth day.

Day 9 – Peacock Green: Maa Siddhidatri

Peacock green is a blue-green shade associated with freshness, individuality and beauty. It closes the widely followed nine-colour sequence on 19 October.

Navratri 2026 Dates: Start, End and Dussehra

Sharad Navratri 2026 is widely listed from Sunday, 11 October to Monday, 19 October, followed by Vijayadashami (Dussehra) on Tuesday, 20 October. The colour calendar is best treated separately from the exact regional tithi schedule because lunar dates can differ by location.

Important Note About Ashtami and Navami in 2026

The nine colour days and the nine tithis should not be treated as identical. In 2026, published Panchang-based calendars can place important tithis such as Durga Ashtami and Maha Navami differently depending on location and sunrise calculations. If you are planning a puja, fast, Sandhi Puja or Kanya Puja, check the Panchang followed in your city or by your family.

What to Wear for Navratri 2026?

You do not need nine new outfits to follow the colour tradition. A dupatta, scarf, blouse, kurta, shirt, saree border, jewellery or even a small accessory in the day's shade can be enough.

  • For home puja: choose comfortable traditional or everyday clothing with the colour as an accent.
  • For Garba or Dandiya: prioritise comfortable footwear and clothing that allows easy movement.
  • If you do not own the exact shade: use the closest colour family or a small accessory.
  • For children: simple colour-coded outfits can make the nine-day tradition fun without requiring expensive purchases.
