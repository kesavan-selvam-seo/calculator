Navratri 9 Colours 2026: Day-Wise List with Dates & Goddess

Updated October 11, 2026

Navratri colours 2026 are orange, white, red, royal blue, yellow, green, grey, purple and peacock green. Sharad Navratri begins on Sunday, 11 October 2026, and the nine day-wise colours run through Monday, 19 October. Vijayadashami (Dussehra) is on Tuesday, 20 October 2026.

Use this quick guide to find the Navratri 9 colours 2026 with dates, the goddess worshipped each day and the commonly associated meaning of each shade. Colour lists can vary by regional or family custom, so treat this as the widely published calendar and check a local Panchang for location-specific puja timings.

What Are the Nine Colours of Navratri?

The widely followed Navratri colour chart for 2026 lists orange, white, red, royal blue, yellow, green, grey, purple and peacock green. Many devotees wear the colour assigned to the day, while others use it in a dupatta, scarf, flowers, rangoli or puja-room decoration. It is a way to take part in the festival’s shared theme; it is not compulsory for everyone.

You may see other colours, including pink, in some Navratri charts. Different regions, organisers and family traditions do not always publish an identical colour sequence. For that reason, use a current-year list and follow the custom familiar to your community.

How Are Navratri Colours Decided?

In a popular colour-calendar tradition, the first colour is associated with the weekday on which Navratri begins. The seven weekday colours are followed by two additional colours to complete the nine-day sequence. Since the festival can start on a different weekday each year, the published order may change.

This weekday-based explanation describes the popular colour-calendar custom, rather than a universal rule observed by every community. In the widely published 2026 list, the sequence begins with orange because 11 October falls on a Sunday.

Navratri Colours 2026: Meaning of Each Colour

Colour meanings are customary interpretations and can differ across sources. They are useful as a simple guide to the symbolism commonly associated with each day.

Day 1 – Orange: Maa Shailaputri

Orange is commonly associated with energy, warmth and enthusiasm. Maa Shailaputri is worshipped on the first day of Navratri and is often connected with strength, devotion and a steady beginning.

Day 2 – White: Maa Brahmacharini

White represents peace, purity and simplicity in popular colour explanations. Maa Brahmacharini is associated with penance, discipline and devotion.

Day 3 – Red: Maa Chandraghanta

Red is associated with courage, strength, passion and devotion. Maa Chandraghanta is revered as a powerful form of the Goddess linked with courage and protection.

Day 4 – Royal Blue: Maa Kushmanda

Royal blue is often linked with confidence, stability and richness. Maa Kushmanda is associated with creation and vitality in devotional traditions.

Day 5 – Yellow: Maa Skandamata

Yellow is commonly used to represent joy, optimism and brightness. Maa Skandamata is worshipped as the mother of Skanda (Kartikeya) and is associated with maternal care.

Day 6 – Green: Maa Katyayani

Green is associated with growth, renewal and harmony. Maa Katyayani represents strength and the power to overcome obstacles.

Day 7 – Grey: Maa Kalaratri

Grey is often interpreted as balance and composure. Maa Kalaratri is a fierce form of the Goddess associated with protection and overcoming fear.

Day 8 – Purple: Maa Mahagauri

Purple is commonly linked with dignity, spiritual depth and fulfilment. Maa Mahagauri is associated with purity, serenity and grace.

Day 9 – Peacock Green: Maa Siddhidatri

Peacock green blends blue and green tones and is often associated with freshness, harmony and beauty. Maa Siddhidatri is worshipped on the ninth day and is linked with spiritual accomplishment.

Navratri 2026 Start Date, End Date and Dussehra

Sharad Navratri’s nine colour days run from Sunday, 11 October through Monday, 19 October 2026. Vijayadashami, also called Dussehra, falls on Tuesday, 20 October 2026. Ghatasthapana (Kalash Sthapana) marks the beginning of the observance on the first day.

Dates for the festival are widely published, but local ritual timings depend on location-specific Panchang calculations. Confirm the muhurat for your city before planning Ghatasthapana or other time-sensitive observances.

What Should You Wear During Navratri 2026?

You do not need to buy nine new outfits. Wear a garment or accessory in the colour of the day, or use the shade in your home celebration if that is easier.

  • For puja at home: choose a simple outfit in the day's colour or add a matching dupatta or scarf.
  • For Garba and Dandiya: incorporate the colour into your outfit while prioritising comfortable clothing and footwear.
  • For work or school: a shirt, ribbon, scarf or small accessory can follow the theme without a full outfit change.
  • For families: use the daily colours for simple group photos or children’s festive activities.
  • If you miss a colour: there is no need to worry. The custom is meant to be joyful and does not determine your devotion.

How to Use the Daily Colours at Home

Bring the colour of the day into your celebrations through flowers, rangoli, fabric, a chunri or other small decorative details. Use whichever items you already have rather than treating the shade as a compulsory puja ingredient.

Frequently Asked Questions About Navratri Colours 2026

What are the 9 colours of Navratri 2026?

The widely followed sequence is orange, white, red, royal blue, yellow, green, grey, purple and peacock green, from 11 to 19 October 2026.

What is the Navratri colour for today, 11 October 2026?

The colour for the first day, Sunday, 11 October 2026, is orange. The day is associated with Maa Shailaputri.

What are the Navratri colours 2026 with dates?

11 October – orange; 12 October – white; 13 October – red; 14 October – royal blue; 15 October – yellow; 16 October – green; 17 October – grey; 18 October – purple; and 19 October – peacock green.

Why do Navratri colours change every year?

In the popular colour-calendar tradition, the sequence starts with a colour associated with the weekday Navratri begins on. Since that weekday changes from year to year, the order can shift.

Are Navratri colours compulsory?

No. Wearing the day's colour is a popular festive custom, not a universal religious requirement. Follow the practices of your family or community.

What if I do not have the exact colour?

A close shade or a small accessory is enough if you choose to follow the custom. There is no need to purchase a new outfit each day.

Can the Navratri colour list vary by region?

Yes. Some published charts and local traditions can differ. Use the colour sequence followed by your family or community, and check a local Panchang for ritual timings.

When is Vijayadashami or Dussehra in 2026?

Vijayadashami (Dussehra) is on Tuesday, 20 October 2026, after the nine Navratri colour days.

What are the nine forms of Goddess Durga?

The Navadurga are Shailaputri, Brahmacharini, Chandraghanta, Kushmanda, Skandamata, Katyayani, Kalaratri, Mahagauri and Siddhidatri.

When should I check Ghatasthapana or puja muhurat?

Check a reliable Panchang for your city because muhurat timings may vary by location. The date alone is not enough to establish the correct local time.
