Navratri Colours 2026: 9 Days, Dates, Colours & Meanings

Updated October 4, 2026

Navratri colours 2026 are orange, white, red, royal blue, yellow, green, grey, purple and peacock green. Sharad Navratri begins on Sunday, 11 October 2026, so the first day's colour is orange. The nine colour days run from 11 October to 19 October, followed by Vijayadashami (Dussehra) on 20 October.

This guide gives the complete Navratri 9 colours 2026 list with dates, weekdays, Goddess Durga forms, colour meanings, how the colours are decided each year, outfit ideas and quick FAQs. The colour tradition is widely followed, but it is not a universal religious requirement; regional and family customs can vary.

What Are the 9 Colours of Navratri 2026?

The nine commonly published Navratri colours for 2026 are orange, white, red, royal blue, yellow, green, grey, purple and peacock green. The sequence is linked to the weekday on which Navratri begins, which is why the order can change from one year to another.

In some yearly colour calendars, a shade such as pink may appear in place of one of the additional colours. For that reason, it is better to check the current year's list instead of copying an older Navratri colour chart.

How Are Navratri Colours Decided Each Year?

The popular Navratri colour tradition begins with the weekday colour associated with the first day of the festival. The first seven colours correspond to the seven days of the week. The remaining two colours complete the nine-day sequence.

Since Navratri lasts nine days, two additional shades are used after the seven weekday colours. In the widely followed 2026 sequence, these are purple and peacock green. Because 11 October 2026 falls on a Sunday, the 2026 chart starts with orange.

Navratri Colours 2026 and Their Meanings

The meanings associated with the colours are cultural and devotional interpretations rather than universal religious rules. They are commonly used to describe the mood or symbolism of each day.

Day 1 – Orange: Maa Shailaputri

Orange is associated with warmth, energy, enthusiasm and brightness. As the opening colour, it gives the first day a lively and positive festive tone.

Day 2 – White: Maa Brahmacharini

White is commonly associated with purity, peace, calmness and simplicity. It is often chosen to reflect a quiet and devotional mood.

Day 3 – Red: Maa Chandraghanta

Red is associated with strength, courage, passion and devotion. It is one of the most prominent colours seen in Navratri clothing and religious decorations.

Day 4 – Royal Blue: Maa Kushmanda

Royal blue is often linked with depth, confidence, richness and stability. Its darker tone gives the fourth day a distinctive festive appearance.

Day 5 – Yellow: Maa Skandamata

Yellow is associated with happiness, optimism, brightness and learning. It is a popular choice for festive clothing and home decorations.

Day 6 – Green: Maa Katyayani

Green represents growth, renewal, harmony and freshness. It is commonly connected with nature and new beginnings.

Day 7 – Grey: Maa Kalaratri

Grey is often interpreted as balanced, grounded and composed. It provides a more understated option compared with brighter festive colours.

Day 8 – Purple: Maa Mahagauri

Purple is commonly associated with dignity, richness, ambition and spiritual depth. It gives the eighth day a distinctive and elegant festive colour.

Day 9 – Peacock Green: Maa Siddhidatri

Peacock green combines blue and green and is associated with freshness, individuality and beauty. It completes the widely followed 2026 nine-colour sequence.

Navratri 2026 Dates: Start, End and Dussehra

Sharad Navratri 2026 begins on 11 October and the nine colour days continue through 19 October. Vijayadashami, also called Dussehra, falls on 20 October 2026. Ghatasthapana marks the beginning of the Navratri observance, but the exact muhurat depends on location.

Do not assume that the colour calendar and every regional puja tithi are identical. Ashtami, Navami and other ritual timings can vary based on local sunrise and Panchang calculations.

What Colour Should You Wear for Navratri 2026?

The simplest way to follow the colour tradition is to wear the colour assigned to that day. You do not need to buy nine complete outfits. A kurta, saree, shirt, dupatta, scarf, blouse, jewellery or another small accessory can represent the day's shade.

  • For home puja: wear the day's colour or use it as a small accent.
  • For Garba or Dandiya: choose comfortable clothing and footwear while incorporating the day's shade.
  • For office celebrations: a shirt, dupatta, scarf or accessory is an easy way to follow the theme.
  • For children: simple colour-coded clothing can make the nine-day tradition engaging without requiring expensive outfits.
  • If you miss the colour: there is no need to worry; the colour tradition is an observance, not a test of devotion.

How to Use Navratri Colours at Home

The colour of the day can be incorporated into more than clothing. Flowers, a chunri, rangoli, candles, diyas, table decorations and small pieces of fabric can all reflect the day's shade.

Navratri Mandir and Puja Decor

You can use fresh flowers, cloth or a chunri in the day's colour around the puja space. Keep the setup simple and respectful rather than treating the colour as a mandatory ritual item.

Rangoli and Entrance Decor

Use the day's colour in rangoli patterns or small decorative elements at the entrance. A close shade is perfectly practical if the exact colour is not available.

Family and Group Celebrations

Families, schools and workplaces can use the daily colour as a simple theme for group celebrations, photos or cultural programmes.

Important Things to Remember

  • The Navratri colour order changes from year to year because the festival starts on a different weekday.
  • The 2026 sequence starts with orange because the first day is Sunday.
  • Regional traditions may use different colour charts or interpretations.
  • Wearing the day's colour is a popular custom, not a universal requirement for Navratri worship.
  • For exact puja muhurat, Ashtami, Navami or Kanya Puja timings, use a location-specific Panchang.
