Indian States and Languages

India is a multilingual country where languages, dialects and scripts often extend across state boundaries. This guide lists major languages commonly spoken or associated with each of the 28 states and explains the difference between a state language, a scheduled language and a language commonly used by communities.

The Indian states and languages topic is useful for school assignments, general knowledge, quizzes and competitive-exam revision. India has 28 states and 8 Union Territories, and its linguistic diversity includes languages from several language families. The list below is a practical GK reference, not an exhaustive list of every language spoken in each state.

How to Read the State-Language List

Major language vs official language

These terms should not be treated as interchangeable. A major language is a language with a substantial or important speech community in a state. An official language is a language adopted by a state through its legal and administrative framework. Article 345 of the Constitution allows a state legislature to adopt one or more languages in use in the state, or Hindi, for official purposes. Therefore, the languages commonly spoken in a state can be more numerous than its official language or languages.

Scheduled language vs state language

The Eighth Schedule of the Constitution lists 22 languages. Being included in the Eighth Schedule does not mean that the language is the sole official language of a particular state. State-level language arrangements are governed separately.

Why one language appears in multiple states

Languages do not follow modern political boundaries. Hindi varieties, Bengali, Marathi, Telugu, Tamil, Kannada, Punjabi, Nepali, Urdu, Santali and many other languages are spoken across more than one state or Union Territory. Migration and long-standing cultural connections also contribute to multilingual cities.

22 Languages in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution

The Constitution's Eighth Schedule currently recognises 22 languages. The official Department of Official Language lists Assamese, Bengali, Bodo, Dogri, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Kashmiri, Konkani, Malayalam, Manipuri, Marathi, Maithili, Nepali, Odia, Punjabi, Sanskrit, Santali, Sindhi, Tamil, Telugu and Urdu. These are constitutional scheduled languages; this category is different from the official-language arrangements of individual states.

Important GK Facts About Indian States and Languages

  1. India has 28 states and 8 Union Territories.
  2. The Eighth Schedule of the Constitution contains 22 languages.
  3. Hindi in Devanagari script is the official language of the Union under Article 343; English may also continue to be used for official Union purposes under the Official Languages Act.
  4. India does not have a single language that is the official language of every state.
  5. State legislatures can adopt one or more languages in use in the state, or Hindi, for specified official purposes under Article 345.
  6. Punjabi is commonly associated with the Gurmukhi script in India.
  7. Hindi, Marathi, Nepali, Sanskrit, Maithili, Dogri and Bodo are commonly written in Devanagari in their standard Indian forms.
  8. Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Gujarati and Odia each have distinctive scripts commonly used for the respective languages.
  9. Many Indian states are multilingual, especially those in the Northeast and states with large urban and migrant populations.
  10. The Census of India 2011 recorded mother-tongue information at India, state, district, tahsil and town levels, demonstrating the depth of India's linguistic diversity.

How to Prepare Indian States and Languages for GK

For quick revision, avoid trying to memorise every language spoken in every state at once. Start with the principal language associations, then learn the 22 Eighth Schedule languages separately. Finally, revise language families and scripts. This three-step method helps prevent confusion between state, language, script and constitutional schedule.

  • Step 1: Learn the 28 states with one or two major language associations each.
  • Step 2: Memorise the 22 Eighth Schedule languages.
  • Step 3: Group languages by family: Indo-Aryan, Dravidian, Austroasiatic and Tibeto-Burman.
  • Step 4: Practise script-based questions such as Hindi–Devanagari and Punjabi–Gurmukhi.
  • Step 5: Recheck official-language questions against current government or constitutional sources when the exact legal status matters.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many languages are included in the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution?

The Eighth Schedule currently includes 22 languages.

Are the 22 scheduled languages the official languages of all Indian states?

No. The Eighth Schedule is a constitutional list of languages. Individual states have their own official-language arrangements, and the languages commonly spoken in a state can be more numerous.

Which language is mainly spoken in Tamil Nadu?

Tamil is the principal regional language of Tamil Nadu. Other languages, including Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam and Urdu, are also spoken by different communities.

Which language is mainly associated with Karnataka?

Kannada is the principal regional language of Karnataka. Tulu, Konkani, Urdu and Kodava are among the other languages spoken in the state.

Which states are mainly associated with Telugu?

Telugu is principally associated with Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, while Telugu-speaking communities are also found in several other parts of India.

Which script is used for Hindi?

Hindi is normally written in the Devanagari script in India.

Which script is used for Punjabi in India?

Punjabi is commonly written in the Gurmukhi script in India.

Why do some states have many languages?

India has long-standing regional, tribal and cultural communities, and languages frequently cross present-day state boundaries. Migration and urbanisation also contribute to multilingual populations.

Is this list of Indian state languages exhaustive?

No. It is a practical GK list of major languages commonly spoken or associated with each state. India has many additional regional, tribal and minority languages.

Where can I verify official language information?

For constitutional and Union-level language information, refer to the Department of Official Language and the Constitution of India. For population and mother-tongue data, refer to Census of India tables.
