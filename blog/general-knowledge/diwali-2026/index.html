When Is Diwali in 2026? Date in India

The main Diwali 2026 date in India is Sunday, 8 November 2026. This date is listed in the India Post 2026 holiday calendar and the government IIRS/ISRO holiday list. Diwali is also written as Deepavali or Deepawali, and many communities celebrate several connected days rather than a single event.

Why can two calendars show different nearby dates? Hindu observances may depend on tithi at sunrise, sunset, moonrise or local ritual practice. Main Diwali 2026 is 8 November, but a nearby Chaturdashi or post-Diwali observance can fall on different civil dates by region.

Choti Diwali 2026: Is It 7 or 8 November?

Choti Diwali 2026 is associated with Narak Chaturdashi, also known as Naraka Chaturdashi, Roop Chaudas or Roop Chaturdashi. In 2026, Chaturdashi tithi begins on 7 November and continues into the morning of 8 November. That is why some greetings calendars mention Saturday, 7 November, while the traditional predawn Abhyanga Snan is observed on Sunday, 8 November in locations such as Delhi, Mumbai and Bengaluru.

Drik Panchang's Mumbai Narak Chaturdashi guide specifically places the 2026 predawn bath on 8 November. If you are planning a puja or ritual rather than a greeting, follow a location-specific panchang and your household tradition.

Common observances include a traditional oil bath, cleaning and decorating the home, lighting lamps and sharing sweets. Traditions vary across states. In parts of southern India, Narak Chaturdashi and early-morning Deepavali celebrations are especially important.

Dev Diwali 2026: Date, Meaning and Celebration in Varanasi

Dev Diwali 2026 is on Tuesday, 24 November 2026. Also known as Dev Deepawali, this festival is celebrated on Kartik Purnima, particularly in Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh. Dev Diwali is not the same day as the main Deepavali celebration on 8 November.

The festival is traditionally associated with the victory of Lord Shiva over Tripurasura and the lighting of lamps along Varanasi's ghats. Visitors often associate it with evening deepdan, illuminated steps along the river and devotional gatherings. According to Drik Panchang's Varanasi calendar, the local 2026 evening deepdan window is approximately 5:08 PM–7:47 PM IST on 24 November. Check local arrangements for events, crowds and any revised timing information.

Main Diwali vs Dev Diwali: Main Diwali is observed on 8 November (Amavasya), while Dev Diwali is on 24 November (Kartik Purnima). The festivals have different calendar bases and traditional associations.

Diwali Puja 2026: Lakshmi Puja Date, Muhurat and Simple Steps

Diwali Lakshmi Puja 2026 is on Sunday, 8 November. Many families perform Lakshmi–Ganesha worship in the evening and light diyas as part of the celebration. The exact auspicious time is city-dependent: it should not be copied from one city and described as valid everywhere in India.

Simple Diwali puja checklist

  1. Prepare the space: clean the puja area, arrange a clean cloth, and place lamps safely away from fabric.
  2. Gather customary items: such as images or murtis of Lakshmi and Ganesha, flowers, rice, sweets, incense and diyas, depending on family practice.
  3. Check the local time: consult the panchang for your city and follow the tradition observed by your household.
  4. Perform the prayers: offer flowers, light the diya carefully and recite prayers or aarti customary in your family.
  5. Share prasad and greetings: spend time with family, friends and neighbours while respecting different ways of celebrating.

This is an introductory cultural overview rather than a fixed religious prescription. Methods, mantras and the appropriate puja sequence differ by sampradaya, region and home tradition.

Why Is Diwali Celebrated? Meaning and Regional Traditions

Diwali is popularly described as the Festival of Lights. Across India, lamps are associated with hope, knowledge, welcome and the victory of light over darkness. Families may clean and decorate their homes, exchange sweets, visit relatives, make rangoli and participate in community gatherings.

One well-known tradition links Diwali with Lord Rama's return to Ayodhya after his exile. Other communities emphasise Goddess Lakshmi, the story of Krishna and Narakasura, or the New Year observances around the festival. Jain communities also mark an important observance associated with Mahavira. Diwali has diverse regional and religious meanings, so no single story describes how everyone celebrates.

The lighting of an earthen diya can be a simple way to mark the evening, but the festival is equally about hospitality, gratitude and making room for others. For students preparing festival assignments or short speeches, that shared message is a thoughtful starting point.

Rangoli Design for Diwali 2026: 8 Attractive and Easy Ideas

Looking for an attractive rangoli design for Diwali 2026? Start with simple shapes and a balanced colour palette. A good rangoli does not have to be large: clean outlines, repeated patterns and a central focal point can make a small entrance look welcoming. These are instructions and decorative examples, not downloadable photographic templates.

🪔
01 / Beginner

Four-diya rangoli

Draw a central circle and four leaf-shaped diyas pointing outward. Fill with maroon and yellow powder; place real lamps at a safe distance from loose materials.

Best for: Small apartment entrances
✿
02 / Floral

Six-petal flower rangoli

Mark a tiny centre and six evenly spaced petals. Outline in white, fill alternate petals with pink and orange, then add a dotted border.

Best for: Easy flower rangoli
❀
03 / Elegant

Lotus rangoli design

Draw a large centre petal, add two curved petals on each side and a green leaf base. Use rose pink, magenta and cream for contrast.

Best for: Puja room threshold
✦
04 / Geometric

Dot-grid rangoli

Place a 5×5 grid of guide dots. Join them into diamond or looping patterns, erase visible guide dots and finish with a coloured outer frame.

Best for: Symmetrical designs
✺
05 / Natural

Marigold and diya ring

Arrange marigold petals in two concentric rings and add small leafy accents. Keep any live flame on a stable, heat-safe surface.

Best for: Flower-petal decoration
✦
06 / Minimal

White kolam-inspired rangoli

Make a compact dot layout and connect dots with flowing white loops. Add a single gold or yellow centre for a modern festive finish.

Best for: Clean, traditional-inspired look
❋
07 / Colourful

Peacock-feather inspired rangoli

Start with a sweeping curved stem, layer teardrop feathers in turquoise, green and blue, and use a darker outline to define the shapes.

Best for: Medium-sized courtyard
✧
08 / Modern

Corner rangoli with lamps

Place one quarter-circle design in an entrance corner. Add repeating arcs, tiny stars and two LED diyas for a space-efficient result.

Best for: Doorstep or balcony corner

How to make any Diwali rangoli design look more attractive

  1. Choose a design size that leaves a clear walking path and does not block doorways.
  2. Outline the main shape lightly before filling with colour, petals or washable materials.
  3. Limit the palette to two or three main colours plus a contrasting outline.
  4. Repeat a simple motif, such as a petal, diya or dot, rather than mixing too many unrelated patterns.
  5. Photograph it from directly above or at a slight angle to show the symmetry.

For an eco-friendly option, use flowers or reusable decorations and clean up leftover materials responsibly. Keep lamps away from small children, pets, flammable powders and trailing clothes. Local traditions such as kolam and alpana have their own histories and styles; these are general design suggestions, not substitutes for those traditions.

Celebrate a Safe, Thoughtful and Eco-Friendly Diwali

Many Diwali traditions can be enjoyed without excessive waste or noise. Choose reusable decorations, use flowers responsibly, prefer LED lighting where suitable, and keep a safe distance between open flames and household materials. If fireworks are used, comply with local restrictions and safety instructions, and be considerate of children, older adults, people with respiratory conditions and animals.

  • Place lit diyas on stable, heat-resistant holders and never leave them unattended.
  • Keep clear routes through doorways, balconies and stairs, even when decorating with rangoli.
  • Use energy-efficient lighting and turn decorations off when they are no longer needed.
  • Share sweets with attention to dietary needs and food allergies where relevant.
  • Celebrate in a way that respects neighbours and the environmental rules in your area.
