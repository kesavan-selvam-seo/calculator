National Symbols of India

India's national symbols represent different aspects of the country's identity, heritage and constitutional traditions. They are common topics in school GK, quizzes and competitive examinations.

The national symbols of India include the National Flag, State Emblem, National Anthem, National Song, National Animal, National Bird, National Flower, National Tree, National Fruit and National Aquatic Animal. Some symbols have a formal constitutional or legal basis, while others are recognised by government sources as national identity symbols.

Details of India's National Symbols

National Flag of India

India's National Flag is a horizontal tricolour of India saffron, white and India green. The white band carries the Ashoka Chakra in navy blue at the centre, with 24 equally spaced spokes. The Flag Code of India specifies a length-to-width ratio of 3:2.

The flag was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 22 July 1947. The design and display of the flag are governed by the Flag Code of India together with applicable laws relating to the national flag.

State Emblem of India

India's State Emblem is an adaptation of the Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath. The emblem shows four Asiatic lions standing back to back, although only three are visible in the two-dimensional representation. The abacus includes representations of the Dharma Chakra and animals, and the motto Satyameva Jayate appears below.

The State Emblem of India was adopted on 26 January 1950. Its use is regulated by the State Emblem of India (Prohibition of Improper Use) Act, 2005 and related rules.

National Anthem of India

Jana Gana Mana, composed by Rabindranath Tagore, is India's National Anthem. The Constituent Assembly adopted it as the National Anthem on 24 January 1950. The official government reference also provides guidance on the playing and singing of the anthem.

National Song of India

Vande Mataram, composed by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay, is India's National Song. It has a distinct historical role in India's freedom movement and is treated separately from the National Anthem.

National Animal of India

The Bengal Tiger (Panthera tigris) is India's National Animal. The tiger is strongly associated with India's wildlife conservation efforts and is found across a range of forest and grassland habitats.

National Bird of India

The Indian Peacock or Indian peafowl (Pavo cristatus) is India's National Bird. Its distinctive plumage and long association with Indian art and culture make it a widely recognised national symbol.

National Flower of India

The lotus (Nelumbo nucifera) is recognised as India's National Flower. It has longstanding cultural and religious significance in India and is commonly used in Indian art and iconography.

National Tree of India

The banyan (Ficus benghalensis) is India's National Tree. Its spreading branches and aerial roots can create a large canopy, making the tree a familiar part of India's landscape and cultural traditions.

National Fruit of India

The mango (Mangifera indica) is widely recognised as India's National Fruit. India has many regional mango varieties, and the fruit has an important place in Indian agriculture, food and culture.

National Aquatic Animal of India

The Ganges River Dolphin (Platanista gangetica) is India's National Aquatic Animal. It inhabits river systems of the Ganges-Brahmaputra-Meghna basin and is an important indicator of river ecosystem health.

National Flag and the Ashoka Chakra

The Ashoka Chakra is a navy-blue wheel with 24 spokes placed in the centre of the white band. The Flag Code of India specifies that the Chakra should be clearly visible on both sides of the flag and provides detailed rules for the flag's design, display and dimensions.

The flag's standard colours are commonly described as India saffron at the top, white in the middle and India green at the bottom. The official Flag Code should be consulted when the question concerns exact display rules rather than basic GK.

National Emblem and the Motto

The State Emblem is derived from the Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath. The motto Satyameva Jayate is taken from the Mundaka Upanishad and means "Truth Alone Triumphs." The emblem is used by the Government of India in official contexts and its unauthorised or improper use is regulated by law.

How to Prepare National Symbols for GK

National symbols are easiest to remember when grouped into categories instead of memorising a random list.

  • Identity: Flag, State Emblem and National Motto.
  • National compositions: Anthem and Song.
  • Wildlife: Bengal Tiger, Indian Peacock and Ganges River Dolphin.
  • Plants and food: Lotus, Banyan and Mango.
  • Visual detail: Remember the 24-spoke Ashoka Chakra and the 3:2 flag ratio.
  • Dates: Revise 22 July 1947 for the Flag's adoption and 24/26 January 1950 for the Anthem and State Emblem.
